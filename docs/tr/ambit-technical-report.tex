\begin{abstract}
Embedding-based systems --- retrieval, retrieval-augmented generation,
deduplication, clustering --- depend on a single geometric property: items
that must be distinguished must remain distinguishable under the similarity
metric. We call the failure of this property \emph{crowding}. It is a local
loss of resolution, invisible to the global statistics that practitioners
routinely examine. This report describes \textsf{ambit}, an
instrument that measures how a corpus occupies its embedding space. It
treats an embedded corpus as a pattern of points on a sphere and asks the
question other sciences ask of point patterns: do points bunch more than
a stated null allows, at which scales, and which points?

The instrument answers the question in its own terms. It measures
occupancy at every scale at once, because binned views change with the
bin. It compares every measurement to a stated reference --- the same
corpus with no crowding --- because a raw similarity number means nothing
on its own. And it carries the diagnosis down to individual documents,
because the useful output is a list of what to fix.

The measurements are stated in retrieval terms. A query never lands
exactly on its target, and when two documents sit close together, a query
aimed at one can return the other --- a \emph{collision}. From pair
distances alone, \textsf{ambit} computes how many collisions to expect at
any level of query imprecision, and reports the largest imprecision the
corpus absorbs before collisions begin: the \emph{resolution bandwidth}
$\sstar$, the corpus's tolerance for imprecise queries.

Validation establishes three properties. On hundreds of corpora with
nothing planted, the instrument alarms only at its advertised rate. It
recovers planted groups of near-duplicates in full --- groups that mean
cosine, isotropy, and hubness miss. And its predictions were tested
blind: across four public retrieval collections and seven public
encoders, every score was computed and locked before the relevance
labels --- the human judgments of which documents answer which queries
--- were consulted. The per-document score picked out in advance the
documents that retrieval then failed on, in every pairing that tests it.
The corpus-level score tracks encoder quality only on similarity-matching
tasks.

The case study embeds one million documents drawn from a public legal
corpus; there, the measurements select interventions and verify them.
Merging exactly the near-duplicate documents the readouts name, at the
similarity level they certify as true duplication, improved twelve
queries and harmed none ($p = 0.0005$); merging at the looser level the
readouts warn against degraded retrieval. Measurement-built guardrails
halve the damage supervised fine-tuning does outside its training
distribution, at no cost to its gains. And a rejection rule fixed in
advance caught a defective fine-tuning round that its own falling loss
endorsed; the labels later confirmed the veto. \textsf{ambit} is
unsupervised --- no labels enter any measurement --- and its core depends
only on \textsf{numpy}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =========================================================================

An embedding model maps items to vectors so that semantic relatedness becomes
geometric proximity. Once a corpus is embedded --- typically onto the unit
sphere $\sphere$, with similarity the cosine of the angle between items ---
every downstream capability reduces to one primitive: \emph{given a direction,
list the items near it}. The primitive degrades in a specific way when
distinct items land too close together: queries cannot separate them, and the
system exhibits impostors at the top of rankings, near-duplicates that cannot
be ordered, and clusters that blur into one another. We refer to this failure
mode as \emph{crowding} and to its complement --- the corpus's ability to
keep distinct items distinct --- as \emph{resolution}.

An embedded corpus is
a \emph{pattern of points on a sphere}, and crowding is a question about
the arrangement of those points: do some of them bunch together more
tightly than a well-spread corpus would allow, at which scales, and which
points are they? Questions of exactly this form are the daily business of
sciences that live on point patterns --- ecologists ask them of trees,
epidemiologists of disease cases, astronomers of galaxies --- and those
fields spent half a century building statistics that answer them at every
scale at once, against stated null models, without bins.
\textsf{ambit}'s design position is that embedding geometry is the same
measurement problem on a new domain, and the report carries that
methodology to the sphere and gives its outputs retrieval meaning
(\S\ref{sec:related} records the transfers).

Seen this way, crowding is difficult to measure for two reasons, which
\S\ref{sec:geometry} and \S\ref{sec:binned} develop: it is \emph{local} (a
collapsing pocket barely moves any global average), and the standard
instruments for localizing density --- histograms, hexbin lattices, voxel
grids, fixed-$k$ neighbor counts --- carry hidden discretization
parameters whose values change the answer. Figure~\ref{fig:introwhy}
previews both difficulties.

The instrument answers the point-pattern question in its own terms. It
measures occupancy at every scale at once, because any binned view lets
the bin choose the answer (\S\ref{sec:binned}). It compares every
measurement to a stated reference --- the same corpus with no crowding,
what statisticians call a \emph{null model} --- so that the benign
concentration every learned encoder exhibits is not mistaken for
pathology (\S\ref{sec:geometry}). And it carries the
diagnosis down to named documents, because the actionable output is a
list of what to fix, not a score.
\S\ref{sec:geometry}--\S\ref{sec:power} build and validate the
instrument under these commitments; \S\ref{sec:training} and
\S\ref{sec:external} use its measurements to drive training,
deduplication, and fine-tuning guardrails.
The contributions --- what \textsf{ambit} adds to the field, beyond the
prior work each part builds on (\S\ref{sec:related}) --- are four.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A calibrated instrument for occupancy and crowding}
  (\S\ref{sec:continuous}, \S\ref{sec:instrument}). The point-pattern
  toolkit of the observational sciences, composed into a single
  instrument for embedding spaces and released as an open, numpy-only
  library. Its readouts, in plain terms: the crowding curve (for every
  similarity level, the fraction of document pairs at least that
  similar); one number summarizing how far that whole curve sits from a
  well-spread reference; a per-document crowding radius that provably
  moves only slightly when the corpus changes slightly (a
  \emph{stability guarantee}); and a catalogue of tight document groups
  found without choosing any similarity cutoff (\emph{threshold-free}).
  Every readout is compared to a stated reference --- a \emph{null
  model} --- including one that absorbs the cone effect of learned
  encoders.
\item \textbf{Retrieval semantics for occupancy measurements}
  (\S\ref{sec:semantics}). An exact conversion from pair distances to
  expected retrieval collisions --- an imprecise query aimed at one
  document returning another --- yielding the resolution bandwidth
  $\sstar$, the corpus's tolerance for imprecise queries, and giving the
  temperature parameter of the widely used uniformity loss
  \cite{wang2020} concrete meaning. This bridge from occupancy
  statistics to retrieval error counts is, to our knowledge, new.
\item \textbf{Blind evidence that geometry predicts retrieval failure}
  (\S\ref{sec:external}). Across four public retrieval collections and
  seven public encoders, with every readout computed and locked before
  the collections' relevance judgments were consulted, the per-document
  collision score picked out the documents that the judgments then
  showed failing at their own queries, in every pairing that tests it
  (sign test $p \approx 2 \times 10^{-7}$). The corpus-level $\sstar$
  tracks encoder quality only where the task is similarity matching ---
  a measured scope law, not a claimed universal.
\item \textbf{Interventions selected and verified by measurement}
  (\S\ref{sec:training}). The readouts choose the repair and the
  verification confirms it, label-free throughout: deduplication at the
  similarity level the readouts certify improved twelve queries and
  harmed none ($p = 0.0005$), while the merge they warn against degrades
  retrieval; guardrails built from the measurements halve the damage
  supervised fine-tuning does outside its training distribution, at no
  cost to its gains; and a rejection rule fixed in advance vetoed a
  fine-tuning round whose own loss curve looked healthy. The training
  penalties behind these carry a guarantee that their gradients act only
  on confusable pairs.
\end{enumerate}

Throughout, \textsf{ambit} is \emph{unsupervised by design}: no labels, gold
pairs, or relevance judgments are consumed. We are explicit about the nature
of the contribution: the mathematics assembled here is, with small
exceptions, classical, drawn from spatial statistics, directional
statistics, geometric inference, and discrepancy theory
(\S\ref{sec:related}). The contribution is an \emph{instrument} --- the
selection, calibration, and composition of known functionals into a
measurement system with stated guarantees, stated nulls, and stated scope
--- together with the negative results that pruned its design space.

\section{Geometry of Crowding}
\label{sec:geometry}
% =========================================================================

This section develops the geometric considerations that the remainder of
the report formalizes. Three elementary observations, taken together, determine the measurement design.

\subsection{Items are directions; retrieval neighborhoods are caps}

Cosine-compared embeddings live on the unit sphere: each item is a
\emph{direction}, and similarity is the angle between directions. A retrieval
neighborhood --- ``everything scoring above $0.8$ against this query'' --- is
a \emph{spherical cap}: the disc of directions within a fixed angle of the
query (Figure~\ref{fig:cap}). This picture reduces every occupancy question
to a statement about caps. Corpus concentration corresponds to caps holding
more items than their share of the sphere's area; an item's degree of
crowding, to the smallest cap around it that already contains other items;
and retrieval confusion, to items sharing a small cap --- \emph{future
collisions}, since a query landing near one cannot avoid the others.

\subsection{In high dimension, ``random'' means ``orthogonal''}

Fix one item on $\sphere$ and picture the set of directions at least
$0.3$-similar to it: a cap around it (Figure~\ref{fig:cap}). In low
dimension that cap is a respectable patch of the sphere. As dimension
grows, the sphere's area migrates toward the thin band $90^\circ$ from
\emph{any} fixed direction --- there are simply ever more ways to be
different --- and the cap's share of the surface collapses: at $d = 768$,
the cap at $\cos \ge 0.3$ holds roughly $10^{-16}$ of the sphere's
area. A uniformly placed second item essentially never lands in it.
Quantitatively, the pair cosine of independent uniform directions
concentrates as $\mathcal N(0, 1/d)$; at $d = 768$ the standard deviation
is $1/\sqrt{768} = 0.0361$, and our measured value on $4{,}000$ sampled
uniform points is $0.0361$ (Figure~\ref{fig:conc}). Three consequences
follow. First, ``similar'' is a geometrically tiny target: two unrelated
items land in each other's $\cos = 0.3$ caps about once in $10^{16}$
draws. Second, the well-spread null's
small-distance tail is \emph{empty}. In each of $19$ simulated
uniform corpora of this size, the fraction of pairs with $\cos \ge 0.30$
is exactly zero, so \emph{any} observed mass there is unambiguous
structure. (Nineteen is the classical envelope count: real data exceeding
all $19$ simulations is a $1/20 = 5\%$ event under the null.) Distance
concentration, the classical curse of high dimension \cite{beyer1999}, is, for a null-calibrated instrument, an asset: every close pair is a finding.
Third, because almost every pair is a near-orthogonal
background pair, \emph{averages over pairs are dominated by the background} ---
the observation the next subsection makes precise.

\subsection{Why averages cannot see crowding}
\label{sec:averages}

Every corpus-wide average of a pairwise quantity lives on the
\emph{square of all pairs}: an $n \times n$ grid with one cell per
ordered pair of items (Figure~\ref{fig:hide}). A group of crowded items
occupies a stretch of the square's \emph{side}; its pairs occupy the
small square block where that stretch meets itself. Side and area scale
differently --- membership is linear, pair share is quadratic --- so a
pocket holding $5\%$ of the items owns only $0.25\%$ of the area over
which any average is taken. The benchmark corpus used throughout this
report makes the dilution concrete. It holds $n = 4{,}000$ items at
$d = 768$, and the last $k = 200$ of them --- five percent --- are
replaced with near-duplicates of one another, so alike (median pairwise
cosine $0.929$) that a retrieval system cannot rank them apart (full
construction in \S\ref{sec:method}). Now follow the arithmetic of the
average, step by step. Five percent of the items yields
$0.05^2 = 0.25\%$ of the pairs, so $99.75\%$ of the average consists of
background pairs, which the previous subsection showed sit near cosine
$0$. Each pocket pair contributes its cosine of roughly $0.93$, weighted
by the pocket's $0.25\%$ pair share, so the entire catastrophe adds
$0.0025 \times 0.93 \approx 0.002$ to the mean. Two hundred documents
became interchangeable, and the corpus-wide average moved from about
$0.000$ to $0.002$: invisible without a calibrated test, and
uninformative about \emph{which} items failed. Covariance-spectrum
summaries (effective rank, isotropy scores) are blind for the same
arithmetic reason: they are sums over the corpus, dominated by the diffuse majority. More generally: \emph{global averages address whether the whole cloud is degenerate; crowding concerns which part of the cloud has collapsed, at what scale, and which items it contains.} A sound instrument must
therefore (i) read \emph{distributions}, not means; (ii) read them
\emph{at every scale}, since pockets live at small scales the bulk never
visits; (iii) \emph{calibrate} against a matched null, since absolute cosine
is not self-interpreting; and (iv) descend to \emph{named items}, since the
actionable output is a list of ids. These four desiderata are the design of
\S\ref{sec:continuous}.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% =========================================================================

The introduction framed an embedded corpus as a point pattern and
crowding as the clustering question the observational sciences ask of
point patterns. Those sciences have mature statistics for answering it;
\textsf{ambit} carries that work to the high-dimensional sphere where
embedded corpora live. Spatial statistics supplies the crowding curve
and its inference; geometric inference, the per-item crowding radius and
the pocket hierarchy; discrepancy theory, the occupancy summary;
directional statistics, the reference that models the cone. Each is
described below in plain terms --- what it does in its home field, and
what changes on embeddings. No donor field supplies the operational
layer: none has a notion of one document outranking another at a query,
so the conversion of pair distances into expected retrieval collisions
(\S\ref{sec:semantics}) is native to this report.

\paragraph{Counting pairs at every distance.}
Ripley's $K$ function \cite{ripley1976,besag1977} is the standard test
for whether a set of points clusters. An ecologist applies it to trees
in a forest plot, an epidemiologist to the home addresses of disease
cases, an astronomer to galaxies in a survey volume, a cell biologist to
proteins imaged on a membrane. The recipe is the same in each: count how
many pairs fall within every distance, and compare those counts to what
scattering the same number of points at random would give. Reading the
comparison as a \emph{function of distance} matters, because a pattern
can be clumped at one scale and evenly spread at another --- trees
clustering into groves while the groves sit apart --- and any single
radius reports one and hides the other.

\textsf{ambit}'s crowding curve is this idea transplanted to the sphere,
and the move is kinder than it sounds. The random-scatter comparison
stays exact: on a sphere, the chance that a random point falls within a
given distance of another is the area of the surrounding patch of
surface, known in closed form (the cap-area law). The transplant also
sheds a nuisance. A forest plot has edges, and a tree near one has
neighbors only on the inside, so its pair counts run low and the
ecologist must compensate (an \emph{edge correction}). A sphere has no
edge --- walk in any direction and the surface continues --- so no such
correction arises \cite{robeson2014}.

\paragraph{Testing a whole curve at once.}
A curve read at every distance invites a multiple-comparison error:
check enough scales and one will look significant by chance. Global
envelope tests \cite{myllymaki2017} test the entire curve as a single
object, and supply \textsf{ambit}'s liftoff test.

\paragraph{Conditioning on an uneven background.}
Spatial statisticians met \textsf{ambit}'s central confound long ago.
Disease cases cluster in cities, but that says nothing about contagion:
people cluster in cities. Raw $K$ reports the crowd, not the outbreak.
The \emph{inhomogeneous} $K$ function \cite{baddeley2000} repairs this
by testing against a background that already contains the uneven
population, so only clustering beyond it is reported. The
anisotropy-conditioned reference of \S\ref{sec:kcurve} is the same move
on the sphere: the cone into which a learned encoder packs every
document is the uneven background, supplied by the angular central
Gaussian of directional statistics, and \textsf{ambit} reports the
clumping the cone does not account for.

\paragraph{What not to transfer.}
The same sciences documented the hazards of partitioned statistics. The
modifiable areal unit problem (MAUP)
\cite{gehlke1934,openshaw1979,openshaw1984} and the ecological fallacy
\cite{robinson1950} are the classical demonstrations that
partition-based statistics change with the partition and say nothing
about individuals --- the two failures, quantified on embedding displays
in \S\ref{sec:binned}, that \textsf{ambit} is built to avoid. The same
lesson appears in one dimension as the histogram's bin-width and origin
sensitivity \cite{scott1979,freedman1981,silverman1986}; the averaged
shifted histogram \cite{scott1985} repairs placement by averaging over
origins, and \textsf{ambit} removes the partition altogether.

\paragraph{Auditing every region at once.}
Stolarsky's invariance principle \cite{stolarsky1973,brauchart2011}
converts an audit that cannot be run directly --- compare actual against
expected occupancy over \emph{every} region at \emph{every} scale ---
into one computable number, the mean pairwise distance.
\textsf{ambit}'s occupancy discrepancy is this theorem put to work.

\paragraph{Scoring individual points, and finding groups.}
The distance to a measure \cite{chazal2011,chazalmichel2021} asks, for
each point, how far it must reach to gather a fixed fraction of the data
--- a robust replacement for density estimation, whose defining property
is stability: small changes to the data provably move every score only
slightly. \textsf{ambit}'s per-item crowding radius is this estimator on
the sphere. For structure, Hartigan's cluster tree
\cite{hartigan1981,chaudhuri2010} reads groups at all densities at once
instead of at one threshold; ToMATo \cite{chazal2013} and HDBSCAN's
condensed tree \cite{campello2015} are two normalizations of that object
\cite{rolle2020}, inheriting stability from persistence theory
\cite{cohensteiner2007}. \textsf{ambit}'s pocket catalogue --- births,
deaths, and prominences of tight groups --- is this machinery with a
null-calibrated reporting floor.

\paragraph{The cone that every transfer must condition on.}
Gao et al.\ \cite{gao2019} and Ethayarajh \cite{ethayarajh2019}
established that learned embeddings do not use the whole sphere: vectors
bunch into a narrow cone, inflating every pairwise cosine at once. This
is \textsf{ambit}'s central confound --- a cone raises similarities
without making any two items confusable --- and the reason every readout
is calibrated against an anisotropy-matched reference rather than the
uniform sphere alone. Mu et al.\ \cite{mu2018} showed that subtracting
the mean direction and a few dominant components restores much of the
usable angular range: the linear repair \textsf{ambit}'s routing
recommends when it attributes crowding to the cone alone. Cai et al.\
\cite{cai2021} found genuine cluster structure \emph{inside} the cone,
which is what the anisotropy-matched reference is designed to isolate.

\paragraph{What the embedding literature measures instead.}
Effective rank \cite{roy2007} and IsoScore \cite{rudman2022} compress
the covariance into one number --- roughly, how many directions carry
variance, and how round the cloud is. \textsf{ambit} reports both as
context, and \S\ref{sec:power} measures that they cannot see small
pockets. Hubness \cite{radovanovic2010}, a few items appearing in
disproportionately many nearest-neighbor lists, is a metric-level
symptom of the same congestion \textsf{ambit} measures geometrically,
and a baseline in the detection-power study. Wang and Isola
\cite{wang2020} decompose contrastive embedding quality into
\emph{alignment} (paired texts land together) and \emph{uniformity}
(everything spreads out); \textsf{ambit} measures only the uniformity
half, being unsupervised, and \S\ref{sec:external} recovers that
decomposition empirically.

\paragraph{Embedding-quality scores.}
RankMe \cite{garrido2023}, $\alpha$-ReQ \cite{agrawal2022}, and their
relatives (surveyed in \cite{tsitsulin2023}) score an embedding, without
labels, by how evenly its variance spreads across directions. On unit
vectors these all re-read the same second-moment spectrum, so they
largely re-parameterize one another; the Vendi score
\cite{friedman2023,pasarkar2024} escapes the family by counting
effective modes through a nonlinear kernel
\cite{giraldo2015,ospanov2024}. \textsf{ambit} takes the lesson as a
design filter (\S\ref{sec:filters}): rather than add another reading of
the spectrum, it measures different objects --- the pair-distance
distribution and per-item fields.

\paragraph{Projections and density.}
Two-dimensional layouts preserve neighborhoods, not density
\cite{wattenberg2016,narayan2021}: a visually dense region of a
projection need not be dense in the embedding, which is why
\textsf{ambit} draws projections but never measures on them. Kernel
density estimation converges at $O(n^{-4/(4+d)})$ --- vacuous at $d$ in
the hundreds \cite{silverman1986} --- which is why the per-item layer
rests on the distance to a measure instead. Local intrinsic dimension
estimators \cite{levina2005,facco2017} are used only per-item and
comparatively.

\section{Experimental Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
% =========================================================================

Every claim marked \emph{measured} in this report follows one protocol,
stated here once. The test environments are scientifically incidental and are described by their properties in \ref{app:env}; the data are what matter.

\paragraph{Datasets.}
Three complementary data regimes carry the evidence; among them, every
claim is testable.

\emph{(i) The synthetic benchmark family --- known ground truth by
construction.} Detection, localization, and recovery claims can only be
scored against a pathology whose membership and scales are known exactly;
planting one supplies that ground truth as \emph{design knowledge} without
the instrument ever consuming labels. The traced benchmark of
\S\ref{sec:averages} is: $n = 4{,}000$ points at $d = 768$ (a contemporary
encoder width), drawn as normalized standard Gaussians (exactly uniform on
$\sphere$); the pocket replaces the last $k = 200$ points ($5\%$) with
$x_i = (c + s\, g_i)/\lVert c + s\, g_i\rVert$ for one shared uniform
direction $c$, i.i.d.\ Gaussian $g_i$, and spread $s = 0.01$. This spread
places the pocket in the near-duplicate regime (median intra-pocket cosine
$0.929$: items a retrieval system cannot rank apart) while leaving every
global average essentially unmoved (\S\ref{sec:averages}), the property
that makes it a stringent test for a local diagnostic. The
uniform control is the same draw without the replacement. The wider family
varies what the traced instance fixes: pocket count ($1$--$20$), size
($20$--$800$), and tightness ($s = 0.005$--$0.06$, spanning near-duplicate
to loose association); mean-shift cones; low-rank collapses; and pure nulls
--- the grid behind the sensitivity, calibration, and power studies. All
generators are stated in full above or beside their experiments; nothing is
tuned to the instrument.

\emph{(ii) A public real corpus --- realistic geometry at scale.} Synthetic
corpora cannot exhibit the learned-embedding pathologies that motivate the
instrument, so the case study (\S\ref{sec:instrument}) uses
\emph{Nemotron-Pretraining-Legal-v1} \cite{nemotronlegal}, a public
(CC-BY-4.0) legal corpus published by NVIDIA: $10^6$ documents drawn from
four of its thirteen subsets --- the California Code of Regulations,
case-law summaries, CaseHOLD, and eCFR question--answer pairs --- embedded
at $d = 1024$ by the Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B instruction-tuned encoder
\cite{qwen3embedding}, L2-normalized, stored as
sharded Parquet with stable document ids. It
supplies what the benchmark cannot: a genuine anisotropy cone (mean pair
cosine $+0.236$), \emph{diffuse} within-subset crowding rather than planted
pockets, subset labels used only to \emph{condition} one readout (never
consumed by a measurement), and corpus scale that exercises the streaming
path.

\emph{(iii) A public evaluation grid --- external outcomes.} The external
study (\S\ref{sec:external}) and the retrieval-facing experiments
(\S\ref{sec:routing}--\S\ref{sec:guardrail}) additionally use four public
retrieval collections with relevance judgments and seven public encoders,
enumerated with citations in Tables~\ref{tab:corpora}
and~\ref{tab:encoders}. Judgments are quarantined: every readout is
computed and hash-frozen before any judgment file is opened, and judgments
are consumed exclusively by scoring code. The three regimes are
deliberately complementary: the benchmark makes claims falsifiable, the
corpus makes them representative, and the grid makes them externally
scored.

\paragraph{Standard settings.}
The following parameters govern statistics defined in
\S\ref{sec:continuous}--\S\ref{sec:semantics}, each introduced fully
where its statistic is defined. Unless an experiment sweeps them: pair statistics use $2 \times 10^5$
sampled distinct pairs; whole-curve inference uses the global rank-envelope
test at $199$ null curves ($99$ inside report figures); the
anisotropy-conditioned reference is sampled from the corpus's own covariance
eigenvalues at matched pair count; the DTM (distance-to-measure) per-item crowding field uses mass fraction
$m = 0.02$; the merge tree uses minimum pocket size $8$; the discrepancy
$z$ uses $64$ null replicates. Each of these defaults is itself validated
by a sweep reported in the section that uses it.

\paragraph{Per-experiment protocols.}
Each experiment below is stated as: the hypothesis under test; the design,
with the reason it takes that shape; the control it is read against; and
the measure that decides. Full parameter values appear beside each result.

\paragraph{Lattice fragility (Table~\ref{tab:maup}).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Hypothesis:} the headline
statistics of binned displays are properties of the lattice, not of the
data.
  \item \textbf{Design:} hold $4{,}000$ synthetic 2-D points fixed and perturb only
the lattice --- shift its origin by fractions of one cell, change its
column count --- because any resulting change can then be charged to the
partition alone.
  \item \textbf{Control:} an isotropic jitter of the \emph{data} by
$0.1\%$ of their span, the honest magnitude of change a data perturbation
of comparable size produces.
  \item \textbf{Measure:} the change in the display's peak
cell count and peak location under each perturbation, lattice against
control.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Confusion-law verification (Table~\ref{tab:flip}).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Hypothesis:} the
closed-form probability that noise flips a ranking is exact in any
dimension.
  \item \textbf{Design:} two fixed unit vectors at a known angle, $3 \times
10^5$ independent noise draws per cell of a $6 \times 4$ grid of angles
and noise scales --- direct simulation of the law's own setting, with no
modeling assumptions to hide in.
  \item \textbf{Control:} the closed form itself; under
the hypothesis the residual is pure Monte-Carlo noise of predictable
size.
  \item \textbf{Measure:} the maximum absolute deviation between empirical flip rate
and formula over the grid.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Redundancy of the confusion and uniformity functionals (Table~\ref{tab:redundancy}).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Hypothesis:} the two functionals may order
corpora identically, making one redundant.
  \item \textbf{Design:} eight synthetic corpus
generators spanning the pathology families, both functionals computed on
each --- redundancy is a claim about \emph{orderings across corpora}, so
the design produces orderings, not values.
  \item \textbf{Control:} perfect rank
agreement, the outcome under full redundancy.
  \item \textbf{Measure:} the rank
correlation and, more importantly, each disagreement, inspected
individually.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Calibration and detection power (\S\ref{sec:power}).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Hypothesis:} the instrument's alarms fire at their nominal rates on healthy corpora
and detect planted pathologies that standard summaries miss.
  \item \textbf{Design:} $400$ pure-null corpora first, to measure false-alarm rates and to set
every detector's threshold at the same $1\%$ specificity --- power
comparisons are meaningless unless all detectors are equally trigger-shy
--- then a grid of planted pathologies, $50$ replicates per cell, with
threshold seeds disjoint from test seeds.
  \item \textbf{Control:} the pure nulls, and
the standard summaries (mean cosine, isotropy scores, effective rank,
hubness, a neighbor-distance quantile) run under identical thresholds.
  \item \textbf{Measure:} empirical false-alarm rate against nominal; detection rate per
pathology cell at matched specificity.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Sensitivity sweeps.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Hypothesis:} every default parameter sits on a
plateau, so reasonable variation does not change conclusions.
  \item \textbf{Design:} sweep each knob (reservoir size, pair-sample size, mass fraction, minimum
pocket size, null-replicate count) one at a time over an order of
magnitude or more.
  \item \textbf{Control:} the readout at the default, with seed-to-seed
spread as the yardstick.
  \item \textbf{Measure:} readout drift across the sweep,
compared to that spread.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The training example (Table~\ref{tab:training}).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Hypothesis:} penalties built from the measurements reduce retrieval collisions without
destroying the embedding's structure.
  \item \textbf{Design:} a planted pocket with a
linear map as the smallest possible trainee, and a $20\%$ slice held out
from mining, weighting, and batching --- verdicts must come from data the
training machinery never touched.
  \item \textbf{Control:} the corpus before training,
and a structure-preservation score against the frozen base.
  \item \textbf{Measure:} held-out flagged-item collisions and neighbor overlap, per schedule.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The encoder loop (\S\ref{sec:loop}).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Hypothesis:} held-out
geometric readouts alone can adjudicate fine-tuning rounds --- accepting
safe ones and rejecting harmful ones --- with no labels.
  \item \textbf{Design:} a fixed
stratified $200{,}000$-document subset with $20\%$ held out, and a
continuation rule registered before any round ran, so acceptance cannot
be negotiated after seeing results.
  \item \textbf{Control:} the base model's readouts;
and the trainee's own loss curve, the signal a practitioner would
otherwise trust.
  \item \textbf{Measure:} per-round held-out $\sstar$, flagged-cohort
collisions, and neighbor overlap against the rule.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Routing (\S\ref{sec:routing}).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Hypothesis:} a repair succeeds only
at the site and scale the measurements prescribe --- not because
deduplication is generically good.
  \item \textbf{Design:} the same label-blind
deduplication executed in all four combinations of two sites (foreign
distractors; the target corpus) and two scales (the confusable window;
the duplicate scale), because only the full $2 \times 2$ separates
``the prescription was right'' from ``any cleanup helps.''
  \item \textbf{Control:} the
three cells the measurements warn against.
  \item \textbf{Measure:} frozen-query failures
fixed and introduced per cell, with an exact paired test.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Guardrails (\S\ref{sec:guardrail}).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Hypothesis:} measurement-derived guard terms prevent the collateral damage of
supervised fine-tuning without reducing its gains.
  \item \textbf{Design:} one supervised
recipe run twice, the arms differing \emph{only} in the guard terms, so
any outcome difference is attributable to them; the held-out measurement
gate is read and recorded before any judgment.
  \item \textbf{Control:} the unguarded
arm.
  \item \textbf{Measure:} the targeted retrieval metric (where no cost is claimed)
and the untargeted open-corpus metrics (where protection is claimed),
plus the pre-label gate's prediction.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The external grid (\S\ref{sec:external}).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Hypotheses:} first, that
each document's expected-collision count predicts which judged-relevant
documents fail at their own queries; second, that the corpus-level
$\sstar$ ranks encoders within a corpus.
  \item \textbf{Design:} every corpus--encoder
cell is embedded and fully measured, and the readouts hash-frozen,
\emph{before} any judgment file is opened --- the freeze makes leakage
from labels into measurements impossible rather than merely disavowed.
  \item \textbf{Control:} for the per-document claim, the natural geometric baseline
(nearest-neighbor cosine); for the corpus-level claim, mean pair cosine.
  \item \textbf{Measure:} per-cell prediction quality (AUC) for the first; per-corpus rank
correlation with nDCG@10 for the second.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Reporting conventions.}
Aggregates are reported with their null value at matched sample size
wherever a null exists; effect sizes are stated in the null's units
($z$-scores) or as ratios. Negative results are reported with the same
prominence as positive ones. Headline benchmark numbers are replicated over
$20$ seeds and reported as mean $\pm$ sd; calibration and power studies use
the replicate counts stated with them ($400$ nulls, $50$ per pathology
cell, $199$-null rank envelopes, $64$-replicate discrepancy nulls);
single-seed values appear only for deterministic or concentration-driven
demonstrations and are marked by the absence of a spread. \emph{Figures}
follow one rule: every data-bearing plot embeds unmodified experimental
outputs as its coordinates.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Real-corpus figures:} the crowding curve, the DTM field,
  and the collision curves plot Nemotron-Legal measurements, and two
  figures reproduce the instrument's own report readouts on that corpus
  natively (the named-document localization list and the pockets bar,
  with the report's values verbatim).
  \item \textbf{Self-computed demonstrations:} the lattice demonstration's
  highlighted peak cells are computed from the figure's own points.
  \item \textbf{Intuition schematics:} the remaining figures carry no
  data and are captioned as schematics.
\end{itemize}

Figure~\ref{fig:maup} and Table~\ref{tab:maup} show both MAUP
effects \cite{openshaw1984} on the tool's own display. The
\emph{zonation} effect is lattice placement: shifting the lattice by
half a cell moves the headline peak count from $310$ to $403$ and
relocates which cell is the peak. The \emph{scale} effect is cell size:
across reasonable column counts the peak count varies by a factor of
$2.4$. Neither perturbation touches the data. The classical
demonstration that re-zoning fixed data can move a correlation across
essentially $[-1,+1]$ \cite{openshaw1979} is the extreme form of the same
phenomenon. Histograms also lose statistical efficiency to discretization: their
mean integrated squared error shrinks as $O(n^{-2/3})$ with sample size,
against $O(n^{-4/5})$ for kernel estimators
\cite{scott1979,freedman1981}. Averaging histograms over many lattice
placements --- the averaged shifted histogram \cite{scott1985} ---
removes the placement dependence and recovers the kernel rate.
\textsf{ambit} retains one hexbin \emph{display}, averaged over
placements in exactly this way and captioned as a view, not a
measurement.

\subsection{One hidden scale each; duplicate blindness}

Crowding is multi-scale: near-duplicate pockets live at tiny angles, topical clusters at moderate ones, the anisotropy cone at large ones. Every bin width,
shell count, and fixed neighbor count $k$ silently commits to one scale.
Injecting $200$ near-identical points --- the benchmark's standard
planted-pocket size, $5\%$ of its $n = 4{,}000$ --- inside an
already-dense cell moves the hexbin peak from $340$ to $540$,
indistinguishable from a benign hot cell
--- while the continuous per-item field of \S\ref{sec:dtm} collapses by a
factor of $18$ at its first percentile. A fixed $k$ in a kNN graph is the
same defect in rank-based form: in a dense pocket $k{=}10$ spans a tiny
radius, while in a sparse outer region it spans a huge one.
``Neighborhood'' therefore denotes a different physical scale at every
point --- precisely the drift a crowding measure must exclude.

\subsection{Steps, nulls, names, and projections}

Four further costs are structural.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Instability.} A bin count is a step function of the data,
  with no guarantee on how far a small perturbation can move it. The
  field of \S\ref{sec:dtm} is provably Lipschitz in the Wasserstein
  distance of the input and measured stable (self-correlation $0.997$
  under jitter at $d{=}768$).
\item \emph{No null.} A peak count carries no statement of what a
  well-spread corpus would show; a perfectly uniform cloud still has a
  hottest cell.
\item \emph{No names} (the ecological fallacy \cite{robinson1950}). A
  cell count cannot say which items are crowded, and the actionable
  output of a diagnosis is a list of ids.
\item \emph{Projection.} Binning a 2-D layout measures the projection,
  not the object: far-apart groups collide in a cell, orthogonal
  crowding vanishes, and nonlinear layouts actively destroy density
  \cite{wattenberg2016}.
\end{itemize}
The repair is not a better ambient density estimate --- kernel density
estimation is vacuous at $d$ in the hundreds \cite{silverman1986} ---
but the abandonment of partitions for scale-free functionals of
interpoint distances, whose guarantees carry no dimension factor.

\section{Continuous Occupancy}
\label{sec:continuous}
% =========================================================================

This section presents the measurement layer: four instruments --- a
whole-curve statistic, a one-number discrepancy, a per-item field, and a
structural tree --- answering, in order, the desiderata of
\S\ref{sec:averages}.
Let $x_1,\dots,x_n \in \sphere$ (unit-normalized; cosine
$c_{ij} = \langle x_i,x_j\rangle$; chord $r_{ij} = \lVert x_i - x_j\rVert =
\sqrt{2 - 2 c_{ij}}$). \textsf{ambit} computes exact full-corpus second
moments by streaming and draws a bounded uniform \emph{reservoir} (a
fixed-size random subsample kept during the single pass) plus a large
random-pair cosine sample for everything else. The pair-sample default
of $2{\times}10^5$ is sized so that the scale readouts of this section
are stable to a few thousandths; the measured concentration rates appear
with the discrepancy statistic below. Throughout this section the \emph{figures} show the real corpus
(Nemotron-Legal, \S\ref{sec:method}) --- the primary examples are drawn from learned-embedding geometry rather than synthetic constructions --- while the traced benchmark of
\S\ref{sec:averages}, whose planted pathology is known exactly, supplies
the falsifiable counterpart for every layer in the text.

\subsection{The crowding curve}
\label{sec:kcurve}

\begin{definition}[Pair-closeness curve]
$K(t) = \Pr_{(i,j)}[\, c_{ij} \ge t \,]$, hereafter the crowding curve: the fraction of distinct pairs at
least $t$-similar, estimated \emph{exactly} from the pair sample by sorting
--- no bins, no origin, no width.
\end{definition}

Recall that Ripley's $K$ function is the spatial statistician's device
for asking whether points cluster: for every radius at once, count the
fraction of pairs that lie within that radius of each other, and compare
the resulting curve against a random scatter. $K(t)$ is this function on
the sphere \cite{ripley1976}, in cosine
coordinates. Two references with matched sample size accompany it
(Figure~\ref{fig:kcurve}):

\emph{(i) The uniform null, tested globally.} For i.i.d.\ uniform points the
pair cosine satisfies $c = 2B - 1$, $B \sim
\mathrm{Beta}\!\big(\tfrac{d-1}{2},\tfrac{d-1}{2}\big)$, so null pair samples
are drawn directly --- no $d$-dimensional vectors are materialized. The sphere is boundaryless, so no edge correction is required and none can be misapplied
\cite{robeson2014}. Inference over the whole curve uses a one-sided global
\emph{rank-envelope} test \cite{myllymaki2017}. The test works by
simulation: the data curve is ranked among $199$ curves drawn from the
null, so the smallest attainable $p$ is $1/200$, and a single global $p$
covers all scales at once. Ties are broken by extreme rank length,
because a one-sided test on $K$ is heavily tied wherever every null curve
is exactly zero. \emph{Liftoff} --- the highest cosine at which the data
exceed the $\alpha$-critical envelope, the null-curve band at the test's
significance level --- is annotated only when the global test rejects. On $40$
pure-null corpora the gated test reported $0$ liftoffs. The naive
pointwise band (the max of the null curves) fired on \emph{every} null seed
at the bulk edge --- a direct demonstration of the multiple-comparison
failure --- and even the $\alpha$-critical envelope alone over-fires
$\approx 4\times$ nominal. The division of labor is therefore strict: the
envelope locates the scale, and the rank $p$ decides significance.

\emph{(ii) The anisotropy-conditioned null.} Real corpora are cone-shaped for
benign reasons; judging them against uniformity alone over-alarms. The second
reference is the \emph{angular central Gaussian} (ACG) of directional
statistics \cite{tyler1987} with the corpus's own
covariance spectrum --- $x = \Sigma^{1/2} g / \lVert \Sigma^{1/2} g\rVert$,
sampled from the eigenvalues alone by rotation invariance: the corpus's
second-moment cone \emph{without} its clustering. Height of the data curve
above this reference is crowding the cone cannot explain.

Figure~\ref{fig:kcurve} shows the curve on the real corpus
(Nemotron-Legal, \S\ref{sec:method}): the global test rejects at the minimum
attainable $p = 1/200$ with liftoff at $\cos \approx 0.81$. The reading is more
detailed than a single scalar: through mid-similarities the data track the ACG reference --- that part of the excess over uniformity \emph{is} the
anisotropy cone, benign and expected of a learned encoder --- while above
$\cos \approx 0.8$ the data rise above both references: genuine
tight-pair structure at the near-duplicate scale, which \S\ref{sec:dtm}
will attribute to named documents. On the traced benchmark the same test
rejects at $p = 1/200$ with liftoff $\cos \approx 0.93$ and a shoulder of
excess mass exactly matching the planted pocket's pair share
($2.4{\times}10^{-3}$) --- the counterpart with known ground truth.
The lower panel of Figure~\ref{fig:kcurve} repeats the reading across
five corpora under one encoder: every corpus carries tight-pair mass far
above the null, but the anatomy differs --- ESCI and TREC-COVID hold the
most near-duplicate mass, Quora the least beyond its paraphrase band ---
structure a single scalar would average away.

\subsection{The occupancy discrepancy (Stolarsky $z$)}

A binned count inspects one cell of one lattice. The complete version
of that question inspects every spherical cap --- every center, every
radius --- and asks how far each cap's occupancy departs from the
uniform expectation. A classical identity collapses this total departure
into a single pairwise average.

\begin{proposition}[Stolarsky \cite{stolarsky1973}]
For points on $\sphere$, the spherical-cap $L_2$ discrepancy --- the squared
deviation between empirical and expected cap occupancy, integrated over
\emph{all} cap centers and \emph{all} cap radii --- equals, up to explicit
constants, a constant minus the mean pairwise chord
$\frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{i,j}\lVert x_i - x_j\rVert$.
\end{proposition}

The mean pairwise chord is therefore ``every lattice at every scale at
once'': the exact completion of what binned occupancy approximates, with no
lattice to choose. \textsf{ambit} reports it as a $z$-score against the
uniform null at matched pair-sample size, pricing in sampling noise.
Measured on the benchmark over $20$ seeds: the uniform control scores
$z = -0.14 \pm 1.00$ --- exactly calibrated --- while planting the $5\%$
pocket moves the mean chord from $1.41405$ to $1.41160$, a shift of
$0.17\%$, which the concentrated null resolves as $z = -47 \pm 4$. Tightening
the pocket further (members within a cap of angular radius $\approx 0.15$)
drives the same statistic to $z = -430$: detection strength scales with the
pathology while the control stays at noise level.

Two properties of $z$ are stated so it is read correctly. First, \emph{$z$
is a test statistic, not an effect size}. Its magnitude grows with the
pair-sample size $P$, because the null mean's spread shrinks as
$1/\sqrt P$. $z$ values are therefore comparable only at matched $P$,
and the instrument reports them at its standard $P = 2{\times}10^5$.
The scale readouts $\sstar$ and liftoff behave like effect sizes
instead: they concentrate as $1/\sqrt P$, with $\mathrm{sd}(\sstar)$
falling from $0.0126$ to $0.0013$ as $P$ grows from $10^4$ to $10^6$. Second, the null's spread must itself be estimated
well: with $24$ null replicates $|z|$ is inflated $\approx 20\%$ and
reproduces with $\mathrm{sd} \approx 8$. The default of $64$ replicates
sits where that inflation has died away; at the default the benchmark
reads $z = -43 \pm 4.5$.

\begin{definition}[Empirical DTM \cite{chazal2011,chazalmichel2021}]
For mass fraction $m$ and $k = \lceil m n\rceil$:
$\dtm_m(x_i) = \big(\tfrac1k \sum_{j=1}^{k} r_{i,(j)}^2\big)^{1/2}$, with $r_{i,(j)}$ the
distance to $x_i$'s $j$th nearest reservoir point --- the radius the item
needs to gather an $m$-share of the corpus.
\end{definition}

A small radius means the item is crowded; a large radius means it is
isolated (Figure~\ref{fig:dtm}). The single parameter $m$ is a
\emph{mass fraction}, scale-free in $n$, and replaces
bin width, grid origin, and fixed $k$ simultaneously. Its one trade-off is
quantified in Table~\ref{tab:mrule}: separation between a
planted pocket and the bulk collapses almost exactly at the predicted blur
boundary $m \approx k/n$, so the reporting rule is ``choose $m$ below the
smallest pocket share you care about,'' and the default $m = 0.02$ resolves
pockets down to $2\%$ of the corpus.

Between the global curve and the per-item field sits \emph{structure}: one
broad pile, or many tight pockets, and who constitutes each?
\textsf{ambit} surfaces the hierarchy its clustering backend already
computes and previously flattened: single linkage on the mutual-reachability
metric (pair distance floored by each endpoint's distance to its own
$k$th neighbor, which suppresses noise bridges) \cite{campello2015}. The
tree is condensed at a minimum pocket size, and pockets are selected by
excess of mass in $\lambda = 1/\mathrm{distance}$: on each branch, the
cluster persisting over the widest scale range is retained. This is
equivalently $H_0$
persistence with the elder rule (at each merge, the younger component
dies) \cite{chazal2013,rolle2020}. Each selected pocket reports birth, death, prominence (death minus
birth: the scale range over which it persists as a separate entity),
size, and member ids
(Figure~\ref{fig:tree}). Three implementation findings mattered --- the
third found by the validation battery itself.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Elder-rule inversion.} The naive elder rule is wrong for
  this purpose: the tightest pocket is the \emph{eldest} component and
  survives to the root, so the bulk ``dies into it.'' Excess-of-mass
  selection resolves the inversion.
\item \textbf{Caterpillar membership.} Single-linkage chains inside a
  tight pocket are caterpillars (long chains with single-node side
  branches). Membership is therefore reported as the condensed core plus
  the side branches that fell out in the tight regime (below the
  geometric mean of birth and death), excluding bulk stragglers.
\item \textbf{The childless root.} Chaining can prevent \emph{any} real
  split from existing: a tight pocket may seed the one component that
  then absorbs the bulk point by point, so no node ever has two children
  of the minimum size, the only cluster is the root, and the tightest
  structure in the corpus goes unreported. A parameter sweep exposed this
  failure --- at minimum size $32$ a planted $200$-member pocket returned
  nothing, and $3$ of $20$ benchmark seeds missed it even at the default.
  The fix treats a childless root as a candidate with death at the final
  merge scale, whereupon the membership rule extracts exactly the pocket
  core.
\end{itemize} Post-fix, the tree recovers the planted pocket in $20$ of $20$ seeds
at exact size ($200.0 \pm 0.0$; birth $0.063$, death $0.898$, prominence
$0.835$) across min-sizes $8$--$64$, and on a two-pocket variant recovers
sizes $60$ and $40$ exactly. Reported prominence also carries a \emph{null
floor}: uniform corpora of matched size and dimension produce maximum
prominences of only $\approx 0.005$ (99th percentile $0.009$ over $400$
nulls), so the instrument simulates the floor per run and suppresses pockets
within it --- a genuine pocket sits two orders of magnitude above it.
Figure~\ref{fig:tree} shows the resulting readout on the real corpus: one
pocket survives the floor ($204$ members, sample ids on its bar), and one
further candidate was suppressed.

\subsection{Complexity, and the design filters}
\label{sec:filters}

All continuous-layer statistics run on the reservoir or the pair sample: the
curve and its nulls are $O(P \log P)$ in the pair-sample size; the DTM field
is a blocked Gram top-$k$, $O(n_r^2 d)$ on a capped subsample (seconds on
CPU); the merge tree is a dense Prim MST on $n_r \le 4{,}096$ rows plus a
linear condensation pass. Full-corpus second moments stream in one pass.

Candidate metrics were admitted by five filters, recorded because they
prune future proposals cheaply:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Spectrum:} does it reduce, under a linear kernel, to the
  covariance spectrum already reported?
\item \emph{Continuity:} is it a continuous functional of the data, or
  a step function of bin, rank, or partition membership?
\item \emph{Null:} does it come with a calibrated reference?
\item \emph{Naming:} can it score individual items, or only regions?
\item \emph{Supervision:} is it label-free?
\end{itemize}
These excluded, among others: RankMe, NESum, and stable rank (spectrum);
$k$-means-conditioned statistics (continuity); raw counts (null); all
cell aggregates (naming); and neural-collapse metrics \cite{papyan2020}
(supervision).

% =========================================================================
\section{Operational Semantics: the Resolution Bandwidth}
\label{sec:semantics}
% =========================================================================

Geometric functionals quantify how uniformly a corpus covers the sphere; a
practitioner needs to know how much query perturbation the corpus can
absorb before retrieval degrades. This section converts the former quantity
into the latter exactly, then measures the conversion.

\subsection{An exact pairwise confusion law}

Model a query aimed at item $x$ as $q = x + \sigma g$, $g \sim \mathcal
N(0, I_d)$ --- the minimal model of a query that intends $x$ but lands
nearby. Retrieval errs on competitor $y$ when $\langle q, y\rangle >
\langle q, x\rangle$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact flip law]\label{prop:flip}
For unit vectors at chord distance $r = \lVert x - y \rVert$,
$\;\Pr[\langle q, y\rangle > \langle q, x\rangle] =
\Phi\!\big({-r}/{2\sigma}\big)$, exactly, in any dimension.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Geometrically (Figure~\ref{fig:bisector}): the set of queries for
which $y$ beats $x$ is the half-space beyond the perpendicular bisector
of $x$ and $y$. The query starts at $x$, at distance $r/2$ from that
boundary, and isotropic Gaussian noise carries it across with
probability $\Phi(-(r/2)/\sigma)$. Algebraically: $\langle q, y -
x\rangle$ is Gaussian with mean $\langle x, y - x\rangle = \cos\theta
- 1 = -r^2/2$ and standard deviation $\sigma \lVert y - x\rVert =
\sigma r$, so the flip probability is
$\Phi\big((-r^2/2)/(\sigma r)\big) = \Phi(-r/2\sigma)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{$C(\sigma)$, $\sstar$, and the uniformity bound}

Summing the law over competitors gives the expected number of items
outranking the intended target,
\[
C(\sigma) = \sum_{j \ne i} \Phi\!\left(-\frac{r_{ij}}{2\sigma}\right)
\;\approx\; (n{-}1)\!\int \Phi\!\left(-\frac{r}{2\sigma}\right) dK(r),
\]
a functional of the pair-distance distribution alone. By the union
bound (Boole's inequality), the sum bounds the probability of \emph{any}
error whatever the correlations among competitor events. $C$ is
therefore conservative: hubness --- a few items recurring as near
neighbors of many others, which correlates those events --- makes the
bound loose, never wrong.

\begin{definition}[Resolution bandwidth]
$\sstar$ is the largest $\sigma$ with $C(\sigma) \le 1$: the most noise
queries can carry before, in expectation, one wrong item outranks the right
one. $C$ is monotone; bisection inverts it.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Uniformity as a Chernoff bound]\label{prop:chernoff}
With $U_t = \log \E\, e^{-t\lVert x - y\rVert^2}$ the uniformity functional
of \cite{wang2020}: $\;C(\sigma) \le (n{-}1)\, e^{U_t}$ at
$t = 1/(8\sigma^2)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\Phi(-z) \le e^{-z^2/2}$ with $z = r/2\sigma$.
\end{proof}

The widely used uniformity temperature is thus not arbitrary: $t = 2$ is a
collision bound at query noise $\sigma = 0.25$. \textsf{ambit} reports
uniformity with this reading attached.

\subsection{Scope}

The guarantee has three boundaries.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Query population.} The corpus is treated as its own query
  population. This is exact for deduplication, clustering, and
  corpus-as-queries retrieval; extrapolating to an external workload
  assumes the workload lands where the documents are.
\item \emph{Noise shape.} The noise channel is isotropic, while real
  query drift is structured. The sensitivity of this assumption is
  measured. Re-ranking a grid of corpus types under structured Gaussian
  channels at matched total power, the isotropic ordering survives
  moderately structured noise (rank-$25$ subspace noise: $0.99$
  pairwise-order agreement over five seeds). It degrades under extreme
  misspecification (rank-$2$: $0.79$; noise aligned with each corpus's
  own top principal directions: $0.82$). Comparative use is therefore
  robust; adversarial noise structure is the stated failure mode.
\item \emph{Aggregation.} The aggregate is an upper bound: $C$
  over-counts confusions, never under-counts them.
\end{itemize}
$\sstar$ is therefore a conservative intra-corpus guarantee, strongest
used comparatively --- one corpus embedded two ways, or one embedding
over time.

Two negative results from the design process are recorded to prevent

\emph{Near-redundancy of $C$ with uniformity}

measurement, the confusion transform adds almost nothing beyond uniformity: rank

approach error probability $1/2$ at every noise scale, while a spread
subspace remains partially navigable, so the confusion ordering is the
operationally correct one. The flip occurs precisely between
qualitatively different pathologies.

with a split-half empirical Wasserstein estimate, hoping for finite-sample
error bars, fails structurally at embedding dimension: the matching distance
between independent halves is of the order of the data scale
($\approx 1.35$ on $\mathbb S^{767}$ at $n_r = 2{,}000$), so the resulting
certificate is $m^{-1/2}\widehat W_2 \approx 13.5$ --- an order of magnitude
beyond the field's entire range. Dimension-free certificates would need a different
metric with its own stability theorem --- an open question, not an
engineering task.

\section{Calibration and Detection Power}
\label{sec:power}
% =========================================================================

The instrument's statistics were calibrated and stress-tested as a
whole against $400$ pure-null corpora: corpora drawn with no planted
pathology, so that any alarm is a false alarm. Four hundred nulls
estimate a $1\%$ false-alarm rate to a standard error near $0.5\%$. The
instrument was then compared for detection power against a suite of
standard diagnostics, with \emph{every} threshold --- the instrument's
and the baselines' --- set on those same nulls at a matched $1\%$
false-alarm rate, so that all detectors are compared at fixed
specificity.

\paragraph{Null calibration.}
On pure nulls ($n = 2{,}000$, $d = 256$; the benchmark family's generators
with no pathology): the global rank test rejects at $p \le 0.01$ in $1.25\%$
of nulls (nominal $1\%$); the gated liftoff fires in $6\%$ at
$\alpha = 0.05$ (nominal $5\%$); $|z| > 3$ occurs in $0.75\%$; and the
pocket-prominence maximum has null median $0.005$ and 99th percentile
$0.009$ --- the floor of \S\ref{sec:tree}. Measured false-alarm rates for
all calibrated thresholds land on $0.010$--$0.013$.

\paragraph{Detection power.}
Table~\ref{tab:power} reports the pathology grid. Three observations follow. First, the continuous layer --- the rank test, $\sstar$, the DTM tail
ratio, and pocket prominence --- is at \emph{full power in every pocket
cell}. That includes the hardest cell, a single pocket of $20$ items
holding $1\%$ of the corpus. On that same cell, mean-cosine ($0.04$),
hubness ($0.18$), and the kNN-distance quantile ($0.22$) sit near the
false-alarm floor: too little of the corpus participates in the pocket to
move any average. This quantifies the claim of \S\ref{sec:averages}.
Second, the exceptions are reported explicitly. Covariance-spectrum
statistics (IsoScore) are \emph{also} at full power on single coherent
pockets: geometrically, a $1\%$ mass sharing one direction lifts an
eigenvalue past the null spectrum edge, so for pure corpus-level
detection of one tight pocket a spectrum summary suffices. What a
spectrum summary cannot do is name the pocket's members, detect a
mean-shift cone (power $0.00$ there, because centering removes the
shift), or express the loss in operational units. Third, the discriminating cells behave as the
theory says they should: the mean-chord $z$ is weak on small pockets (their
pair mass is tiny); the pocket detector correctly declines to alarm on a
cone ($0.12$ --- a cone is not a pocket); and hubness is blind to a
low-rank collapse. The layer's value is therefore not a single more powerful
scalar but full power at matched specificity \emph{together with}
localization to named items, scale attribution, and operational units ---
the properties the baselines structurally lack.

\section{The Instrument}
\label{sec:instrument}
% =========================================================================

\subsection{Architecture}

\textsf{ambit} is a library with a thin CLI. One canonical in-memory type
(\texttt{EmbeddingSet}) flows through a pipeline: a \emph{streaming scan}
(one pass over \texttt{.npy/.npz/.parquet/.csv/.jsonl}, single file or
sharded directory) accumulates exact second moments, norms, and a uniform
reservoir; a context builder derives projections (PCA by default), the
eigenspectrum, a kNN graph (optionally filtered to \emph{reciprocal}
neighbors, which suppresses hubs by construction), unsupervised clusters,
and the pair-cosine sample. All measurements of
\S\ref{sec:continuous}--\S\ref{sec:semantics} are library functions over
these objects; a separate presentation layer (Appendix~\ref{sec:report})
renders them. The core depends on \textsf{numpy} alone; heavier capabilities
(tabular IO, ANN graphs, UMAP, GPU paths, on-the-fly embedding via an
OpenAI-compatible endpoint, the training extra) are optional and degrade
gracefully. A comparison mode aligns two embeddings of the same items by id
and computes a local neighbor-overlap view read against global
representational-similarity statistics (centered kernel alignment, CKA \cite{kornblith2019}, kernel and
rigid distances); the local/global pair can disagree informatively --- a high-CKA, low-overlap outcome indicates an
embedding change that preserves gross structure while rewriting fine
neighborhoods.

mean cosine $\approx 0$): an approximate scan's reservoir is only as
representative as the row order that fed it, so the instrument's approximate
mode is for exploratory iteration rather than for conclusions on data whose layout may
correlate with content.

\section{From Measurement to Gradient}
\label{sec:training}
% =========================================================================

The preceding sections establish that the instrument detects, localizes,
and prices crowding. This section and the next test a stronger claim: that the measurements
suffice to \emph{drive} interventions. Four interventions are tested:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a measurement-derived training objective (below);
\item a measurement-gated fine-tuning loop on the case-study encoder
  (\S\ref{sec:loop});
\item execution of the instrument's deduplication prescription
  (\S\ref{sec:routing});
\item measurement-derived guardrails for supervised fine-tuning
  (\S\ref{sec:guardrail}).
\end{enumerate}
The confusion kernel is differentiable, so the measurement layer doubles as
a training-time regularizer with a stated guarantee.

\subsection{Losses and the gradient-locality guarantee}

For a batch $z_1,\dots,z_B$ (normalized) and a \emph{measured} target scale
$\sigma$:
\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{conf}}
= \mathop{\mathrm{mean}}_{(i,j) \notin \mathcal E}
  \Phi\!\left(-\frac{\lVert z_i - z_j\rVert}{2\sigma}\right),
\qquad
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pres}}
= \mathop{\mathrm{mean}}_i \;
  \mathrm{KL}\!\left(p^{\mathrm{base}}_i \,\middle\|\, p_i\right),
\]
where $\mathcal E$ excludes positive and guarded pairs and $p_i$
($p^{\mathrm{base}}_i$) are temperature-softmax similarity rows under the
tuned (frozen base) model. $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{conf}}$ is the batch
estimate of $C(\sigma)$; since $\partial\Phi(-r/2\sigma)/\partial r \propto
e^{-r^2/8\sigma^2}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:gradwin}), its gradient vanishes
exponentially for pairs beyond $\approx 3\sigma$: far-pair (dissimilarity)
structure \emph{cannot} be disturbed, a property enforced in the test suite
(a near pair receives $>10^3\times$ the gradient of an orthogonal pair).
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pres}}$ is the neighbor-overlap comparison in
differentiable form --- the explicit statement of ``do not rewrite who is
similar to whom.'' (Implementation note: the chord computation floors
$2 - 2c$ before the square root; at exactly zero the root's infinite
derivative poisons masked entries with $0 \cdot \infty$ NaN gradients.)

\subsection{Mining, protocol, and measured effect}

Batch composition is driven by the measurements in three ways.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Sampling:} items are drawn with weight proportional to
  their per-item collision counts, with a uniform floor so that the
  uncrowded bulk still anchors the geometry.
\item \emph{Negatives:} candidate negatives are drafted from the
  \emph{confusable window} --- the cosine range between the measured
  liftoff, where confusability begins, and the duplicate scale, where
  identity begins (operationalized as $\cos \approx 0.98$ throughout).
\item \emph{False-negative guard:} an anchor's top-$m$ base-model
  neighbors are never used as negatives, because the confusable window
  is precisely where unlabeled true relatives live.
\end{itemize} The protocol is staged.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Diagnose}, and take the cheapest fix that the readouts
  license: a cone licenses linear post-processing; duplicates license
  data repair; only genuine beyond-cone clustering licenses training.
\item \emph{Train} with $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{task}} + \lambda_c
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{conf}} + \lambda_p \mathcal L_{\mathrm{pres}}$
  at the measured $\sigma$.
\item \emph{Verify} on a held-out reservoir never seen by mining, and
  never grade with the functional that was optimized.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Closing the loop on a full-scale encoder}
\label{sec:loop}

The synthetic example establishes the mechanism; the remaining question is
whether the loop --- measure, construct the objective from the measurements,
train, re-embed, re-measure --- operates on a contemporary public encoder and a million-document corpus, with the measurements adjudicating every round. We ran this loop on
the case-study corpus (\S\ref{sec:instrument}) and its $0.6$-billion-parameter
instruction-tuned encoder. A fixed, stratified $200{,}000$-document
measurement subset (proportional across the four subsets) was drawn once;
$20\%$ was held out and never touched by mining, sampling weights, or
training. The objective is fully unsupervised: $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{conf}}$
at the subset's measured $\sstar = 0.134$ under the false-negative guard,
plus $\lambda_p \mathcal L_{\mathrm{pres}} $ against frozen base-model
references, with batches composed half from collision-weighted sampling and
half from guarded confusable-window pairs (the confusable window of \S\ref{sec:training}: liftoff $0.769$ to $0.98$). Between rounds, a pre-registered licensing rule
decides continuation: $\sstar$ must not fall and held-out neighbor overlap must remain at least $0.90$ --- that is,
tuning may rewrite at most one held-out neighbor in ten.

Table~\ref{tab:loop} summarizes the rounds; three findings follow. First,
\emph{the instrument rejects a misspecified round}. The initial LoRA round
computed $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pres}}$ against stored embeddings of full
documents while the trainer embedded token-truncated views, so the gradient
was dominated by a length distillation (the model learning to map
truncated views onto full-document embeddings); training losses fell while every
global readout degraded ($\sstar$ down, cohort collisions up $17\%$, the
pocket enlarged). The licensing rule caught this from the held-out
measurements alone. Second, with references matched to the trainer's own
views and the confusable window placed in every batch, both LoRA and full
fine-tuning are \emph{safe but marginal}: no readout degrades, held-out
overlap stays at $0.97$, and the flagged cohort improves by $2$--$3\%$ in
the median. The effect is small but nearly universal across items: of the $408$
flagged items that fall in the held-out split, $406$ (LoRA) and $356$
(full fine-tune) individually reduce their expected collisions (paired
sign tests, $p < 10^{-100}$ and $p < 10^{-56}$). $\sstar$ is measured as genuinely unchanged rather than
noisily so: the paired difference across eight independent pair samples is
$+0.00007$ against a per-sample standard deviation of $0.00012$. All of
this despite the full fine-tune moving roughly $40\times$
the parameters of the LoRA round on a doubled pair batch. Third, the capacity ladder --- linear adapter, LoRA, full fine-tune ---
shows that \emph{capacity is not the binding constraint}. The linear
adapter, which halved collisions on the synthetic single-pocket benchmark
(Table~\ref{tab:training}), is itself rejected on the real corpus:
$\sstar$ falls and cohort collisions worsen by $9\%$, because a single
linear map cannot serve the corpus's mixture of scales. LoRA and the full
fine-tune land within noise of one another. The corpus's dominant
pocket is textual near-duplication, and the preservation term correctly
refuses to separate items whose inputs are near-identical. This is the
training-side confirmation of the staged protocol's routing rule: duplicate
pathology is a data-repair problem, and no training objective that preserves
base-model semantics can substitute for deduplication. Training at the
measured scale is safe; it is not a shortcut around the data.

As a final check that the subset verdicts transfer, the full corpus was
re-embedded with the accepted fine-tune and measured at full scale with the
identical pipeline as the base case study. Every readout reproduces within
measurement noise --- $\sstar\ 0.1227$ against the base $0.1226$, liftoff
$0.817$, effective rank $728$, IsoScore $0.089$, mean pair cosine $0.235$,
hubness skew $1.88$, top pocket $198$ items at prominence $0.090$ against
the base $204$ at $0.090$ --- so the fine-tune that improves retrieval
(below) is confirmed geometry-preserving at $10^6$ documents, and the
$200$k held-out measurement is a faithful proxy for the full corpus.

Training loss alone endorsed that round; only the held-out geometric readouts identified the regression. The right panel shows the gate that caught it: the rejected LoRA round is the

not the (marginal) improvement of the accepted rounds; it is that on every round that the labels later scored, the held-out geometric verdict agreed in sign with the label-based evaluation, at zero label cost.

\subsection{Driving retrieval behavior}
\label{sec:driving}

The loop above never touches a relevance judgment; judgments enter exactly
once, after all rounds are frozen, to \emph{score} the frozen predictions
(the blind-then-score discipline of the validation plan). On the corpus's
citation-grounded question-to-section retrieval set ($1{,}000$ held-out
questions against a $35{,}173$-section sub-corpus, exact search), the
unsupervised full fine-tune moves recall@1 from $0.288$ to $0.301$ and
MRR@10 from $0.467$ to $0.475$; the LoRA round is flat ($0.286$, $0.466$).
The recall gain does not reach significance at this query count: McNemar's
exact test on the paired outcomes gives $p = 0.45$ ($118$ against $131$
discordant queries), and the bootstrap interval on the MRR@10 difference,
$[-0.008, +0.023]$, spans zero. The correct reading is therefore
\emph{retrieval-neutral-to-positive}: a per-item-significant geometry
improvement whose aggregate task effect is bounded near zero, consistent
with the duplication finding --- most failures are not caused by the
crowding the objective can legally repair.

For context, a label-supervised
fine-tune measured under the same harness reaches recall@1 $0.355$, an
upper reference point that supervision, not geometry, supplies.

The per-item layer makes a sharper, item-level
prediction: documents with high base-model expected collisions should be the
ones that \emph{fail at their own queries} (a judged-relevant document
outranked by non-relevant neighbors). Scored blind, base expected-collision
counts predict failure participation at AUC $0.578$ against $0.551$ for the
natural control (top-$1$ neighbor cosine) over $953$ gold documents. The
signal is significantly better than chance (bootstrap $95\%$ interval
$[0.536, 0.622]$) but the margin over the proximity control is not
individually significant on this single corpus ($+0.028$, paired bootstrap
interval $[-0.017, +0.073]$) --- establishing the cross-corpus version of
this comparison is a designated hypothesis of the preregistered
external-validation plan.

In operational terms the instrument drives retrieval behavior through the
readout-to-intervention map exercised end to end in this section: the
anisotropy layer licenses linear post-processing; named near-duplicate
pockets route to data repair before any training; genuine beyond-cone
clumping licenses guarded training at the measured $\sstar$, verified on
held-out measurements with a pre-registered continuation rule; per-item
collision counts triage which documents to repair or re-chunk first; and
$\sstar$ itself sets the query-noise budget an application may spend.

\subsection{Executing the routing rule}
\label{sec:routing}

We tested the routing claim directly, executing the repair that the
measurement prescribed while blind to all labels, then scoring on the
frozen evaluation. The repair has three mechanical steps:
\begin{enumerate}
\item identify near-duplicate groups purely from geometry (pairs above a
  stated cosine threshold, grouped by connectivity);
\item reduce each group to one canonical member, chosen by identifier
  order;
\item map relevance judgments through that reduction mechanically.
\end{enumerate} Two repair sites and two thresholds give a double dissociation
(Table~\ref{tab:routing}): each repair helps at exactly one
site-and-scale combination and harms or does nothing at the others.
Removing \emph{foreign} near-duplicates inside the confusable window
(the liftoff scale) fixes $12$ queries and breaks none (McNemar exact
$p = 5 \times 10^{-4}$); this is the significant retrieval gain that
training could not deliver. Merging \emph{within} the formulaic target
corpus at that same scale is significantly harmful ($57$ queries
broken): these regulatory sections share boilerplate structure, so their
confusable window contains legally distinct sections. Only merging at
the true-duplicate scale ($\cos \ge 0.95$) helps within the corpus. The instrument's scale structure --- liftoff marks
the onset of confusability, not the duplicate boundary --- prescribes
different repairs at different scales, and the held-out labels confirm each
mapping in both directions (Figure~\ref{fig:routingfig}).

\subsection{Guardrails for supervised fine-tuning}
\label{sec:guardrail}

The strongest test of the training layer reverses its role: rather than
driving an unsupervised objective, the measurements \emph{guard} a
label-supervised one. A prior supervised fine-tune of the case-study
encoder had gained first-rank retrieval while silently collapsing an untargeted multi-hop evaluation. This is
exactly the failure class that $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pres}}$ and the
false-negative guard exist to prevent. We ran the identical supervised recipe (in-batch contrastive loss on
$45{,}465$ question--section pairs, two epochs) in two arms differing
only in the guardrails.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Control:} the task loss alone.
\item \emph{Guarded:} the task loss plus $\lambda_p \mathcal
  L_{\mathrm{pres}}$ against the frozen base on identical truncated
  views. In addition, any batch document whose base-model cosine to the
  anchor's positive exceeds the measured liftoff is masked out of the
  contrastive denominator, so the task loss never pushes apart pairs
  that the base model considers confusable relatives.
\end{itemize}

The held-out measurement gate, read before any judgment, ranked the arms:
the guarded model dominates on every label-free readout (neighbor overlap
$0.650$ against $0.537$; $\sstar$ $0.146$ against $0.136$, the base being
$0.134$; flagged-cohort collisions $0.47$ against $2.10$). The frozen
evaluations then confirmed the prediction. On the targeted task the
guardrails cost nothing (MRR@10 $0.503$ against $0.501$; both arms far
above the base $0.467$). On the untargeted open evaluation the guarded arm wins everywhere.
Single-document MRR@10 is $0.597$ against $0.563$. The decisive metric is
all-gold@10 --- the fraction of multi-hop queries whose every
judged-relevant document reaches the top ten --- because that is where
the historical regression lived. There the guarded arm scores $0.339$
against the control's $0.223$, a $52\%$ relative improvement over
$1{,}496$ held-out queries; the gap exceeds five standard errors. The
control reproduces the historical collapse; the guard halves the damage
while improving every other number. Two conclusions follow: the
measurement-derived guardrails make supervised domain adaptation safer at
no cost to its gains, and the instrument's label-free held-out gate
selects the better model before any label is consulted.

task where relevance \emph{is} geometric proximity --- and there the naive
mean-cosine control is \emph{inverted} ($-0.79$). On product search the
correlation is null ($-0.07$; graded shopping relevance mixes attribute
matching with intent the geometry cannot see), and on open-domain QA and
biomedical search it reverses ($-0.54$, $-0.32$): the smallest encoder
attains the best geometry and the worst retrieval. The mechanism is the alignment--uniformity decomposition observed
directly. Retrieval quality factors into two ingredients: semantic
alignment (does the encoder place related texts near each other?) and
geometric headroom (does the space leave room to tell neighbors apart?).
\textsf{ambit} is unsupervised by construction, so it measures only the
second. $\sstar$ therefore ranks encoders only where alignment is
essentially constant across them, and a semantically weak encoder can
attain excellent geometry by spreading apart documents it does not
understand. The
operational conclusion: the corpus-level
$\sstar$ is a \emph{budget} for comparative use --- the same corpus under
encoder variants, repair, or drift, as the guardrail gate used it --- not
a leaderboard; the externally validated cross-encoder readout is the
per-item collision field above.

\textbf{Ground truth for the scale semantics.} On the duplicate-question
collection, whose judgments annotate true duplicates, predicting
``duplicate'' from pair cosine alone yields precision that rises
monotonically along the threshold ladder ($0.29$ at $\cos 0.80$, $0.93$ at
$0.95$, $0.98$ at $0.98$ for the case-study encoder), while recall at the
duplicate scale is low because annotated duplicates are mostly
paraphrases living in the confusable window. This is item-level
confirmation of the two-scale reading --- confusable onset versus duplicate
boundary --- that the routing experiment (\S\ref{sec:routing}) turned into
opposite repair prescriptions. Figure~\ref{fig:dedupf1} plots the ladder.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
% =========================================================================

\textbf{External validation.} A first cross-corpus slice now exists
(\S\ref{sec:external}): the per-item claim is confirmed in every
informative cell and the corpus-level claim received a measured scope law.
The full preregistered study remains open --- more collections (one large
multi-hop corpus is deferred with its queue preserved), encoder-degradation ladders, preregistration, and independent replication --- and the
scope law itself is a hypothesis to re-test: the alignment--uniformity
split predicts that adding any alignment probe would restore ranking power
on knowledge-limited tasks, but such a probe requires supervision the tool
deliberately excludes. The confusion transform makes one falsifiable prediction that its
near-redundant sibling does not
(Table~\ref{tab:redundancy}): duplicate pathology outranks diffuse
collapse.
\textbf{Query distributions.} Accepting an optional \emph{unlabeled} query
set would replace the corpus-as-queries assumption with a measured cross-set
confusion, within the tool's supervision constraint.
\textbf{Sharper aggregates.} The union bound is safe but loose where hubs
live; Gaussian comparison inequalities may yield two-sided estimates.
\textbf{Recoverability.} ``Resolution headroom'' --- the fraction of
measured crowding removable by a budgeted transform class, computed rather
than predicted --- would attribute measured crowding to the embedding model
versus the data themselves.
\textbf{Streaming structure.} The pair curve sketches trivially; the merge
tree does not; a mergeable approximate hierarchy with prominence error
bounds would extend the structural layer past the reservoir.

\section{Conclusion}
% =========================================================================

Crowding is a failure mode that embedded systems exhibit without overt
errors, and it is exactly the
property that global averages cannot see and binned displays cannot be
trusted to measure. The instrument described here rests on one methodological
commitment applied uniformly: \emph{read continuous objects at every scale
against stated nulls, and descend to named items}. The mathematics is
classical --- Ripley's curve, Stolarsky's identity, the distance to a
measure, the condensed cluster tree, a Gaussian tail bound --- and the
contribution is their selection, calibration, and composition into a system
with stated guarantees and stated scope, traced end to end through a single
benchmark whose planted pathology every layer detects, localizes, prices,
and (in the training layer) partially repairs. The negative results are
reported alongside the positive ones because an instrument's reliability rests in part on a precise statement of its
limits.

\section{Engineering Notes}
\label{app:eng}
% =========================================================================

The implementation is organized so that its asymptotic costs match the
claims of \S\ref{sec:instrument}: one streaming pass over the corpus,
followed by statistics whose cost is independent of corpus size. This
appendix records the implementation decisions behind the measured constants
of Table~\ref{tab:scaling}.

\paragraph{Streaming scan.}
Ingestion is a single sequential pass over raw \texttt{.npy} files
(memory-mapped) or sharded Parquet (chunked decode), in batches of
$5\times10^4$ rows. Each batch of $b$ rows updates the running mean, the
norm moments, and the $d \times d$ second-moment scatter through one
$X_b^{\!\top} X_b$ GEMM, and feeds a bounded reservoir sample; peak memory
is $O(d^2 + \text{reservoir})$ irrespective of corpus size, and the
covariance spectrum is extracted once at the end of the pass. An optional
approximate mode terminates the pass after a prescribed number of rows and
estimates the spectrum from that prefix; \S\ref{sec:instrument} documents
the failure mode this introduces on sorted shards.

\paragraph{Blocked kernels at reservoir scale.}
No computation materializes an $m \times m$ matrix for reservoir size $m$.
Top-$k$ cosine values (the input to the distance-to-measure field) and
collision counts are computed from $b \times m$ Gram tiles with
partial-selection top-$k$ (\texttt{argpartition}, linear per row, in place
of a full sort). The mutual-reachability minimum spanning tree uses a
vectorized Prim iteration that maintains a frontier-distance array:
$O(m^2)$ arithmetic executed as full-array operations, $O(m)$ memory
beyond the current tile. The structural layer additionally caps its inputs
($6{,}000$ points for the DTM field, $4{,}096$ for the merge tree), which
fixes the corpus-size-independent constants observed in
Table~\ref{tab:scaling}.

\paragraph{Null sampling without vector synthesis.}
All isotropic-null quantities exploit the closed form for the pair cosine
of independent uniform directions on $S^{d-1}$,
$c = 2B - 1$ with $B \sim \mathrm{Beta}\!\left(\tfrac{d-1}{2},
\tfrac{d-1}{2}\right)$: null pair samples are drawn directly from this
scalar law, so a $199$-replicate rank envelope requires $199$ scalar-array
draws rather than $199$ synthetic corpora. The anisotropy-conditioned
reference uses the rotation invariance of the pair-cosine law under the
angular central Gaussian --- only the covariance spectrum enters --- so
reference pairs are generated from a small pool of spectrum-weighted
Gaussian directions with no eigenvector computation. The Gaussian
confusion law is evaluated with a vectorized rational approximation to
$\operatorname{erf}$ (absolute error below $1.5\times10^{-7}$), which
keeps the core free of dependencies beyond \textsf{numpy}.

\paragraph{One shared pair sample.}
A single random-pair cosine sample (default $2\times10^5$ pairs) is drawn
once per corpus and reused by every pairwise statistic --- $K(t)$, the
envelope test, the discrepancy $z$, the confusion functional $C(\sigma)$,
and $\sstar$. The continuous layer therefore costs one pass over a
fixed-size array per statistic, and all reported statistics refer to the
same measured sample.

\paragraph{Distributed training and pair-batch statistics.}
The encoder-loop trainer (\S\ref{sec:loop}) runs data-parallel: each rank
embeds its own rows and the embeddings are gathered across ranks (the local
slice retaining its gradient) before the pair losses, so pair statistics
scale with world size at fixed per-device memory. This matters beyond
throughput: the pair losses see only within-batch pairs, and small pair
batches permit batch-level overfitting --- the model separates the pairs it
is shown while degrading out-of-batch structure, a failure the loop's
held-out measurements catch. Gathered batches must be identically shaped on
every rank; the batch builder emits a fixed count of distinct rows per rank
for this reason.

\paragraph{Optional acceleration tiers.}
The core is \textsf{numpy}-only, with the heavy kernels routed through
multi-threaded BLAS. An optional module provides the same three kernels
--- covariance accumulation, pair cosines, and exact blocked $k$NN --- on
\textsf{torch} devices (CPU, CUDA, or Apple \textsf{mps}, with automatic
selection), transferring each chunk to the device as it streams; an
approximate-neighbor path through FAISS is available where installed.
These tiers change constants, not asymptotics; every number reported in
this paper was produced by the \textsf{numpy}-only configuration
(\ref{app:env}).

\section{Formula Glossary}
\label{app:glossary}
% =========================================================================

Every formula in the report is restated here in plain language: what it
formulates, why it matters, how it is used, and what every symbol and
operation means. Entries follow the order of first appearance. The first
entry explains the shared notation that recurs throughout; each later entry
still lists all of its own symbols.

\paragraph{How to read the notation (used throughout).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $n$ --- the number of documents in the corpus. $d$ --- the number
  of coordinates in each embedding vector (the ``dimension'').
  \item $i, j$ --- indices naming individual documents, each running from
  $1$ to $n$. Writing $x_i$ means ``the embedding vector of document number
  $i$''; a pair $(i,j)$ names two different documents.
  \item $\Pr[\,\text{event}\,]$ --- ``the probability that the statement
  inside the brackets is true.'' A subscript names what is random: in
  $\Pr_{(i,j)}[\cdots]$ the random thing is the choice of pair --- draw two
  distinct documents at random, and ask how often the bracketed statement
  holds. With no subscript, the randomness is stated in the entry (for the
  flip law it is the query noise).
  \item $\{\sigma : C(\sigma) \le 1\}$ --- ``the set of all values $\sigma$
  such that the condition after the colon holds''; $\max\{\cdots\}$ takes
  the largest member of a set.
  \item $\sum_{j \ne i}$ --- add up the expression that follows, once for
  every allowed value of the index ($j$ running over all documents except
  $i$). $\frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{i,j}$ --- add over all $n^2$ ordered pairs,
  then divide by their count: an average over pairs.
  \item $\int f(r)\, dK(r)$ --- an average of $f$ weighted by how often
  each distance $r$ occurs among pairs; ``$dK(r)$'' means ``the fraction of
  pairs whose distance is (approximately) $r$.''
  \item $\E$ --- the expected value (average) over the stated randomness.
  \item $\log$ --- the natural logarithm; $e^{x}$ --- the exponential
  function, its inverse.
  \item $\partial f / \partial r$ --- the derivative of $f$ with respect to
  $r$: how fast $f$ changes when $r$ changes a little. $\propto$ ---
  ``proportional to'': equal up to a constant factor that does not depend
  on the variable under discussion.
  \item $\lVert v \rVert$ --- the Euclidean length of a vector $v$ (square
  root of the sum of its squared coordinates). $\langle u, v \rangle$ ---
  the dot product of two vectors (multiply matching coordinates, add them
  up).
  \item $\Phi$ --- the standard normal cumulative distribution function:
  $\Phi(u)$ is the probability that a standard Gaussian (bell-curve) random
  number falls below $u$. $\Phi(0) = 1/2$; $\Phi(-4) \approx 3 \times
  10^{-5}$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Unit vectors and cosine similarity: $c_{ij} = \langle x_i, x_j
\rangle$, points on $\sphere$.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it says:} Every document is represented as a
  length-one arrow in $d$-dimensional space, and the similarity of two
  documents is the cosine of the angle between their arrows.
  \item \textbf{Why it matters:} Cosine is the score retrieval systems
  actually rank by, so all geometry in the report is expressed in the same
  units the system uses.
  \item \textbf{How it is used:} Every statistic in the report is a
  function of these pairwise cosines.
  \item \textbf{Term by term:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $x_i$ --- the embedding vector of document $i$, rescaled to
    length $1$ (each of its $d$ coordinates is a number; the whole vector
    is one point in $d$-dimensional space).
    \item $\sphere$ --- the unit sphere in $d$ dimensions: the set of all
    vectors of length exactly $1$. The superscript $d-1$ is the standard
    name for the sphere's surface (a globe in 3-D space has a 2-D surface);
    nothing in the report depends on this convention.
    \item $\langle x_i, x_j \rangle$ --- the dot product of the two
    vectors, which for length-one vectors equals the cosine of the angle
    between them.
    \item $c_{ij}$ --- shorthand for that cosine between documents $i$ and
    $j$: $+1$ means identical direction, $0$ unrelated (perpendicular),
    $-1$ opposite.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Chord distance: $r_{ij} = \lVert x_i - x_j \rVert = \sqrt{2 - 2
c_{ij}}$.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it says:} The straight-line distance between two
  points on the sphere, computed directly from their cosine.
  \item \textbf{Why it matters:} The query-noise law below is exact in
  chord distance, not in cosine, so the report converts between the two
  constantly; this identity is the dictionary.
  \item \textbf{Term by term:}
    \begin{itemize}
    \item $x_i - x_j$ --- the vector pointing from document $j$'s position
    to document $i$'s.
    \item $\lVert x_i - x_j \rVert$ --- that vector's length: the ordinary
    straight-line distance between the two points.
    \item $r_{ij}$ --- shorthand for that distance.
    \item $\sqrt{2 - 2c_{ij}}$ --- the same distance computed from the
    cosine alone. It follows by expanding the squared length: $\lVert x_i -
    x_j \rVert^2 = \lVert x_i \rVert^2 + \lVert x_j \rVert^2 - 2 \langle
    x_i, x_j \rangle = 1 + 1 - 2 c_{ij}$.
    \item Reference points: identical items have $r = 0$; perpendicular
    ones $r = \sqrt 2 \approx 1.41$; opposite ones $r = 2$.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The null cosine scale: coordinates $\sim \mathcal N(0, 1/d)$,
typical cosine $\approx 1/\sqrt d$.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it says:} If points are spread uniformly over a
  high-dimensional sphere, each coordinate behaves like a Gaussian random
  number with variance $1/d$, and the cosine between two random points is
  almost always within a few multiples of $1/\sqrt d$ of zero (at $d =
  768$, about $\pm 0.036$).
  \item \textbf{Why it matters:} This is the baseline against which
  ``suspiciously similar'' is judged: in high dimension, unrelated items
  are \emph{very} nearly perpendicular, so even modest cosines are
  extremely surprising under the null.
  \item \textbf{Term by term:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\sim$ --- ``is distributed as'': the quantity on the left
    behaves like a random draw from the distribution on the right.
    \item $\mathcal N(0, 1/d)$ --- the normal (bell-curve) distribution
    with mean $0$ and variance $1/d$: values concentrate near zero, spread
    $\approx 1/\sqrt d$.
    \item $1/\sqrt d$ --- the typical size of a null cosine; it shrinks as
    dimension grows, which is the concentration phenomenon.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Pair-closeness curve: $K(t) = \Pr_{(i,j)}[\, c_{ij} \ge t\,]$.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it says:} For every similarity threshold $t$
  simultaneously, the fraction of document pairs that are at least
  $t$-similar.
  \item \textbf{Why it matters:} It is the report's primary object: a
  complete, bin-free record of how much of the corpus sits at every
  closeness scale. Crowding appears as an excess of very close pairs ---
  the curve ``lifting off'' above its reference at high $t$.
  \item \textbf{How it is used:} Estimated exactly by sorting a large
  random sample of pair cosines; compared against the two null curves
  below; the comparison is tested with the rank-envelope test.
  \item \textbf{Term by term:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $K$ --- the name of the curve (after Ripley's $K$ function in
    spatial statistics, of which this is the spherical, cosine-coordinate
    version). For each input $t$ it returns one number between $0$ and
    $1$.
    \item $t$ --- a similarity threshold, swept continuously from low to
    high; the curve is read at every $t$ at once, which is what ``no bins''
    means.
    \item $(i, j)$ --- a pair of two distinct documents, chosen uniformly
    at random from the corpus.
    \item $\Pr_{(i,j)}[\, c_{ij} \ge t\,]$ --- the probability, over that
    random choice of pair, that the pair's cosine reaches the threshold ---
    equivalently, simply the fraction of all pairs that do.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The uniform null for pair cosines: $c = 2B - 1$ with $B \sim
\mathrm{Beta}\!\big(\tfrac{d-1}{2}, \tfrac{d-1}{2}\big)$.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it says:} If points were spread perfectly evenly on
  the sphere, the cosine of a random pair would follow this exact, known
  distribution --- a Beta distribution stretched from $[0,1]$ to $[-1,1]$.
  \item \textbf{Why it matters:} It gives the ``perfectly spread corpus''
  reference curve in closed form: no simulation of fake corpora is needed
  (one draws random \emph{numbers}, not random \emph{vectors}), which is
  what makes $199$-replicate null bands cheap.
  \item \textbf{Term by term:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $c$ --- the pair cosine whose null distribution is being stated.
    \item $B$ --- a random draw from the Beta distribution, which produces
    numbers between $0$ and $1$.
    \item $\mathrm{Beta}(a, a)$ with $a = (d-1)/2$ --- a two-parameter
    family of distributions on $[0,1]$; with both parameters equal it is a
    symmetric bell around $1/2$ that narrows as dimension grows (the
    concentration phenomenon again).
    \item $2B - 1$ --- rescales $[0,1]$ onto $[-1,1]$ so the result is a
    cosine: $B = 1/2$ (the typical draw) maps to $c = 0$, i.e.\
    perpendicular.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The anisotropy-matched reference (ACG): $x = \sqrt{\lambda}
\odot g \,/\, \lVert \sqrt{\lambda} \odot g \rVert$.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it says:} A second reference corpus that copies the
  real corpus's overall \emph{shape} --- its elongation along some
  directions and flattening along others --- but contains no clustering
  whatsoever.
  \item \textbf{Why it matters:} Learned embeddings almost always occupy a
  narrow cone (the ``cone effect''), which inflates all cosines benignly.
  Comparing the data against this reference, instead of against the
  uniform null alone, separates crowding the cone explains (benign) from
  crowding it cannot (genuine pockets).
  \item \textbf{Term by term:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $g$ --- a vector of $d$ independent standard Gaussian random
    numbers: a direction chosen with no preference at all.
    \item $\lambda$ --- the corpus's covariance eigenvalues: a list of $d$
    non-negative numbers, one per principal direction, each saying how much
    variance the corpus has along that direction. Together they describe
    the corpus's ellipsoidal shape.
    \item $\sqrt{\lambda} \odot g$ --- multiply each coordinate of $g$ by
    the square root of the matching eigenvalue ($\odot$ is coordinate-wise
    multiplication); this stretches the isotropic random cloud into the
    corpus's ellipsoid.
    \item dividing by $\lVert \cdots \rVert$ --- rescale to length one, so
    the reference point lands on the sphere like the real data.
    \item Only the eigenvalues enter --- rotating a corpus changes none of
    its pairwise cosines, so the eigen\emph{vectors} are irrelevant here.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The global rank-envelope test and the liftoff cosine.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it says:} To decide whether the data curve $K(t)$
  genuinely exceeds the null band --- across \emph{all} thresholds at once,
  not cherry-picking one --- the test simulates many null curves, ranks the
  data curve among them at every threshold simultaneously, and returns one
  global $p$-value. The \emph{liftoff cosine} is the smallest similarity at
  which the data curve exits the envelope, reported only when the global
  test rejects.
  \item \textbf{Why it matters:} Reading exceedance off a pointwise band at
  each threshold separately commits a multiple-comparison error --- on pure
  noise it fires essentially always (measured: every one of $40$ null
  corpora). The global test's false alarms match its nominal rate.
  \item \textbf{How it is used:} Every liftoff claim in the report is gated
  on this global $p$; extreme-rank-length (ERL) ordering breaks ties among
  simulated curves.
  \item \textbf{Term by term:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item envelope --- the band spanned by the simulated null curves
    (here $199$ of them, drawn via the Beta law above).
    \item $p$-value --- the probability, if the corpus were truly
    well-spread, of seeing a curve as extreme as the data's anywhere along
    its length. Small $p$ (e.g.\ $0.005$) means ``a well-spread corpus
    essentially never looks like this.'' With $199$ simulations the
    smallest attainable $p$ is $1/200 = 0.005$.
    \item ERL (extreme rank length) --- the tie-breaking rule: when two
    curves are equally extreme at their worst point, the one that stays
    extreme over a longer stretch ranks as more extreme.
    \item liftoff cosine --- the onset similarity of genuine tight-pair
    structure: below it the data track the reference; above it they exceed
    the envelope.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Stolarsky invariance and the occupancy discrepancy $z$:
$\text{cap discrepancy} = \text{const} - \frac{1}{n^2} \sum_{i,j} \lVert
x_i - x_j \rVert$.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it says:} Take every possible ``cap'' on the sphere
  (every center, every radius), compare how many points each cap holds
  against how many it should hold, square the errors, and add them all up.
  Stolarsky's theorem says this total --- which looks uncomputable ---
  equals a constant minus the average distance between pairs of points.
  \item \textbf{Why it matters:} It is the exact completion of what binned
  occupancy displays approximate: \emph{every} lattice at \emph{every}
  scale simultaneously, with no lattice to choose. A corpus that
  under-spreads (crowds anywhere, at any scale) has a smaller average
  pairwise distance.
  \item \textbf{How it is used:} The report computes the mean pairwise
  chord and standardizes it against the uniform null, giving the occupancy
  discrepancy $z$. On the benchmark, uniform controls score $z \approx 0$
  and the planted pocket scores $z = -47$.
  \item \textbf{Term by term:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item cap discrepancy --- the total squared error between actual and
    ideal cap occupancy, accumulated over all cap centers and radii; the
    formal object on the left of the identity.
    \item const --- an explicit constant that depends only on $n$ and $d$,
    not on where the points are; it plays no role in comparisons.
    \item $\frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{i,j} \lVert x_i - x_j \rVert$ --- the average
    chord distance over all ordered pairs of documents (sum over every
    pair, divide by the number of pairs).
    \item $z$ --- the reported score: the observed mean chord minus the
    null mean, divided by the null standard deviation, where the null mean
    and standard deviation come from redrawing the same number of pair
    cosines from the Beta law. $z = -47$ reads ``the corpus's average
    distance sits $47$ standard deviations of sampling noise below the
    well-spread value.''
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Distance to a measure: $\dtm_m(x_i) = \big( \tfrac1k
\sum_{j=1}^{k} r_{i,(j)}^2 \big)^{1/2}$ with $k = \lceil m n \rceil$.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it says:} For each item: the typical distance it must
  reach to gather the nearest $m$-fraction of the corpus around it --- a
  per-item crowding radius. Small radius $=$ crowded; large radius $=$
  isolated.
  \item \textbf{Why it matters:} It descends from corpus-level statistics
  to \emph{named items}, which is what makes findings actionable, and it is
  provably stable: outliers and small perturbations of the corpus move it
  only slightly (the Wasserstein-stability guarantee).
  \item \textbf{How it is used:} Computed for every reservoir item; its low
  tail (the most crowded items) is compared against the uniform corpus's
  band; the report lists the most-crowded and most-isolated items by
  identifier.
  \item \textbf{Term by term:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\dtm_m(x_i)$ --- the function's name (``distance to the
    measure'') applied to document $i$ at mass fraction $m$; one number
    per document.
    \item $m$ --- the mass fraction, the method's one knob (e.g.\ $0.02$:
    gather $2\%$ of the corpus); being a fraction makes it scale-free in
    corpus size.
    \item $k = \lceil m n \rceil$ --- that fraction converted to a count of
    neighbors; $\lceil \cdot \rceil$ rounds up to a whole number.
    \item $r_{i,(j)}$ --- the distance from item $i$ to its $j$th nearest
    neighbor; the parenthesized subscript $(j)$ means the distances have
    been sorted ascending, so $(1)$ is the closest.
    \item $\tfrac1k \sum_{j=1}^{k} r_{i,(j)}^2$ --- the average of the
    squared distances to the $k$ nearest neighbors.
    \item $(\cdots)^{1/2}$ --- the square root of that average: a
    root-mean-square radius, an average robust to any single neighbor.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The merge tree: mutual reachability, birth, death, prominence.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it says:} Connect items by their shortest bridges
  (single linkage) under the \emph{mutual-reachability} distance. As the
  connection scale grows, tight groups form and later dissolve into the
  bulk. A pocket's \emph{birth} is the scale at which it first holds
  together, its \emph{death} the scale at which it merges into something
  larger, and its \emph{prominence} $=$ death $-$ birth is how long it
  exists as its own entity.
  \item \textbf{Why it matters:} Prominence replaces every flat clustering
  threshold: a pocket is reported not because it passed a cutoff but
  because it persists across a wide range of scales. A null-calibrated
  prominence floor (the $99$th percentile of prominence produced by pure
  noise at matched size and dimension) suppresses spurious pockets.
  \item \textbf{Term by term:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item single linkage --- build connections by repeatedly merging the
    two closest groups; the merge tree records the order and scale of
    every merge.
    \item mutual reachability of $a, b$ --- $\max\{d(a,b),
    \mathrm{core}(a), \mathrm{core}(b)\}$: the largest of the three listed
    quantities, where $d(a,b)$ is the ordinary distance between the two
    points and $\mathrm{core}(x)$ is the distance from $x$ to its own
    $k$th nearest neighbor. Taking the max means two points count as close
    only if each is also locally dense --- a noise point far from
    everything cannot form a spurious bridge.
    \item birth / death --- the connection scales at which a group first
    assembles and at which it is absorbed into an older group (the
    \emph{elder rule}: when two groups meet, the younger one dies).
    \item prominence --- death minus birth, in distance units: the width of
    the scale range over which the pocket is a separate entity.
    \item excess of mass --- the selection rule choosing, on each branch of
    the tree, the cluster that persists longest.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The query-noise model: $q = x + \sigma g$, $g \sim \mathcal N(0,
I_d)$.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it says:} A query aimed at item $x$ lands nearby
  rather than exactly on it: $x$ plus Gaussian noise of scale $\sigma$ in
  every direction.
  \item \textbf{Why it matters:} It converts geometry into operational
  risk. Real queries paraphrase, truncate, and typo; $\sigma$ is the
  abstract knob for all such imprecision, and every downstream quantity is
  a function of it.
  \item \textbf{Term by term:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $q$ --- the query vector the system actually searches with.
    \item $x$ --- the item the query ``means'': its intended target.
    \item $g \sim \mathcal N(0, I_d)$ --- a fresh standard Gaussian vector:
    each of the $d$ coordinates is an independent bell-curve draw with mean
    $0$ and variance $1$ ($I_d$, the identity matrix, encodes
    ``independent, unit variance, no preferred direction'').
    \item $\sigma$ --- the noise scale: how far, typically, the query lands
    from its target, in the same units as chord distance. $\sigma = 0$
    means perfect queries; larger $\sigma$ means sloppier ones.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The exact flip law: $\Pr[\langle q, y \rangle > \langle q, x
\rangle] = \Phi(-r / 2\sigma)$.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it says:} The probability that a noisy query aimed at
  $x$ scores a competitor $y$ higher than $x$ itself depends on nothing but
  the distance between $x$ and $y$, through one evaluation of the normal
  tail. Exactly --- in any dimension.
  \item \textbf{Why it matters:} It is the bridge between geometry and
  retrieval errors, and it explains why near-duplicates are catastrophic:
  as $r \to 0$ the probability approaches $\Phi(0) = 1/2$ --- a duplicated
  document loses half its queries to its twin no matter how small the
  noise is.
  \item \textbf{How it is used:} Verified by direct simulation to
  Monte-Carlo precision (max deviation $9 \times 10^{-4}$); summed over
  competitors to give $C(\sigma)$; used as the training loss kernel.
  \item \textbf{Term by term:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $x$, $y$ --- the query's intended target and one competing
    document, both unit vectors; $q$ --- the noisy query from the previous
    entry.
    \item $\langle q, y \rangle$ --- the dot product of query and
    competitor: exactly the similarity score retrieval ranks by. The
    bracketed statement $\langle q, y\rangle > \langle q, x\rangle$ reads
    ``the competitor scores higher than the intended target,'' i.e.\ the
    retrieval errs.
    \item $\Pr[\cdots]$ --- the probability of that error over the
    randomness of the query noise $g$.
    \item $r$ --- the chord distance between $x$ and $y$; $\sigma$ --- the
    query-noise scale.
    \item $\Phi(-r/2\sigma)$ --- the normal tail evaluated at
    $-r/2\sigma$. Why this argument: the set of points scoring $x$ and $y$
    equally is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining them,
    which sits at distance $r/2$ from $x$; Gaussian noise of scale $\sigma$
    crosses a boundary $r/2$ away with probability $\Phi(-(r/2)/\sigma)$.
    Larger separation or smaller noise pushes the argument more negative
    and the error probability toward zero.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Expected collisions: $C(\sigma) = \sum_{j \ne i} \Phi(-r_{ij} /
2\sigma) \approx (n{-}1) \int \Phi(-r/2\sigma)\, dK(r)$.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it says:} For an average item, the expected number of
  wrong competitors that a noisy query would rank above it: add the flip
  probability over every competitor.
  \item \textbf{Why it matters:} By Boole's inequality (the probability
  that \emph{any} of several events occurs is at most the sum of their
  individual probabilities), $C(\sigma)$ bounds the probability of
  \emph{any} retrieval error, no matter how the competitors are correlated
  --- so it is conservative: crowding can make it loose, never wrong.
  \item \textbf{Term by term:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $C(\sigma)$ --- the function's name: expected collision count at
    noise scale $\sigma$; one number per $\sigma$, increasing in $\sigma$.
    \item $i$ --- the item being queried; $j$ --- each competing document
    in turn.
    \item $\sum_{j \ne i} \Phi(-r_{ij}/2\sigma)$ --- the flip probability
    against competitor $j$ (previous entry), added up over all $n-1$
    competitors.
    \item $(n-1) \int \Phi(-r/2\sigma)\, dK(r)$ --- the same sum computed
    from the pair-distance distribution alone: average the flip probability
    over the distribution of pair distances (that is the integral against
    $dK$), then multiply by the number of competitors. This is why a
    random pair \emph{sample} suffices --- no per-item computation is
    needed for the corpus-level curve.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Resolution bandwidth: $\sstar = \max\{\sigma : C(\sigma) \le
1\}$.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it says:} The largest amount of query noise the corpus
  can absorb before, in expectation, one wrong item outranks the right one.
  \item \textbf{Why it matters:} It is the report's headline operational
  scalar: a noise \emph{budget} in retrieval units. Comparing $\sstar$
  against the same corpus size spread uniformly (the ratio reported
  alongside it) says what fraction of the theoretically available budget
  the embedding retains.
  \item \textbf{Term by term:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\{\sigma : C(\sigma) \le 1\}$ --- the set of all noise scales at
    which the expected collision count stays at or below one; $\max$ takes
    the largest such scale.
    \item the threshold $1$ --- ``one expected collision per item'': the
    point where retrieval starts, on average, to lose. It is a convention
    (a tolerance), and the report states it wherever $\sstar$ appears.
    \item $C$ is monotone --- more noise always means more collisions ---
    so this largest admissible $\sigma$ is well defined and is found by
    bisection: repeatedly halve an interval known to contain the crossing.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Uniformity as a collision bound: $U_t = \log \E\, e^{-t \lVert x
- y \rVert^2}$ and $C(\sigma) \le (n{-}1)\, e^{U_t}$ at $t = 1/(8
\sigma^2)$.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it says:} The widely used Wang--Isola ``uniformity''
  loss --- the log of the average of $e^{-t r^2}$ over pairs --- is, at the
  right temperature, exactly the logarithm of a Chernoff upper bound on the
  collision count $C(\sigma)$.
  \item \textbf{Why it matters:} It gives the popular loss operational
  units: its temperature knob $t$ is not folklore but a choice of
  query-noise scale ($t = 2$ corresponds to $\sigma = 0.25$). It also
  explains when uniformity misleads --- it is a bound, and the report
  measures where the bound's ordering and the exact functional's ordering
  disagree.
  \item \textbf{Term by term:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $U_t$ --- the uniformity functional's value at temperature $t$
    (one number per $t$; more negative $=$ more spread).
    \item $x, y$ --- a random pair of documents; $\E$ --- the average over
    that random pair.
    \item $e^{-t \lVert x-y \rVert^2}$ --- a Gaussian-shaped penalty: $1$
    when the pair coincides, decaying with squared distance at rate $t$
    (large $t$ = only very close pairs are penalized).
    \item $\log$ --- the natural logarithm of the average, following the
    original definition.
    \item $t = 1/(8\sigma^2)$ --- the dictionary between temperature and
    noise scale; where the identity holds.
    \item the proof, in one line --- the normal tail bound $\Phi(-z) \le
    e^{-z^2/2}$ (a Chernoff bound) applied to $z = r/2\sigma$ turns each
    flip probability into $e^{-r^2/8\sigma^2}$, and summing gives
    $(n{-}1)\, e^{U_t}$.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The global suite: mean pair cosine, IsoScore, effective rank,
hubness skew.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it says:} Four whole-corpus summaries used as context
  and as baselines. Mean pair cosine: the average $c_{ij}$ over random
  pairs (how coned the corpus is). IsoScore: how close the covariance is to
  perfectly isotropic --- equal variance in all directions --- on a
  $0$-to-$1$ scale. Effective rank: $\exp$ of the entropy of the
  normalized eigenvalue distribution --- roughly, how many directions carry
  meaningful variance (between $1$ and $d$). Hubness skew: the skewness
  (asymmetry) of the count of how often each item appears in others'
  $k$-nearest-neighbor lists --- heavy right skew means a few ``hub'' items
  intrude into everyone's neighborhoods.
  \item \textbf{Why it matters:} These are the statistics that practitioners
  already consult; the detection-power study shows them blind to small
  pockets that the continuous layer detects at full power, because they
  are global averages.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Training losses: $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{conf}} = \mathrm{mean}_{(i,j)
\notin \mathcal E}\, \Phi\!\big({-\lVert z_i - z_j \rVert}/{2\sigma}\big)$
and $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pres}} = \mathrm{mean}_i\,
\mathrm{KL}\big(p_i^{\mathrm{base}} \,\|\, p_i\big)$.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it says:} Two differentiable objectives built from
  the measurements. The first is the batch version of the collision count:
  the average flip probability over batch pairs, at the \emph{measured}
  noise scale, excluding a set of protected pairs. The second pins local
  semantics: for each anchor, the distribution ``who in this batch is
  similar to me'' under the model being trained must stay close to the
  same distribution under the frozen base model.
  \item \textbf{Why it matters:} $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{conf}}$ spends
  gradient only where confusion actually lives, and $\mathcal
  L_{\mathrm{pres}}$ prevents the repair from destroying meaning. Their
  balance ($\lambda_p$) is the loop's central tension: recovery against
  fidelity.
  \item \textbf{Term by term:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\mathcal L$ --- a loss: a number training tries to make small;
    the subscripts name the two losses (confusion, preservation).
    \item $z_i$ --- the embedding of batch item $i$ under the model
    currently being trained (it changes every step; $x_i$ is reserved for
    the fixed corpus vectors).
    \item $\mathrm{mean}_{(i,j) \notin \mathcal E}$ --- average over all
    ordered batch pairs \emph{except} those in the excluded set; $\notin$
    means ``not a member of.''
    \item $\mathcal E$ --- the excluded pairs: known positives and the
    false-negative guard (each anchor's top-$m$ base-model neighbors,
    which are likely unlabeled true relatives and must not be pushed
    apart).
    \item $\Phi(-\lVert z_i - z_j \rVert / 2\sigma)$ --- the flip law
    applied to the batch pair at the measured scale $\sigma = \sstar$.
    \item $\mathrm{KL}(p \,\|\, q)$ --- the Kullback--Leibler divergence:
    the standard measure of how much a distribution $q$ misrepresents a
    reference distribution $p$; zero exactly when they agree, larger the
    more they differ. The double bar $\|$ is part of the notation and
    separates reference from candidate.
    \item $p_i$ and $p_i^{\mathrm{base}}$ --- for anchor $i$, the softmax
    over its cosines to the rest of the batch (exponentiate each cosine
    divided by a temperature of $0.05$, then normalize to sum to one ---
    low temperature sharpens the distribution toward the nearest
    neighbors), computed under the trained model and under the frozen base
    model respectively.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Gradient locality: $\partial \Phi(-r/2\sigma) / \partial r
\propto e^{-r^2 / 8\sigma^2}$.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it says:} The derivative of the flip probability with
  respect to pair distance dies off as a Gaussian in $r$: pairs farther
  apart than about $3\sigma$ receive exponentially negligible gradient.
  \item \textbf{Why it matters:} It is a structural safety guarantee:
  minimizing $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{conf}}$ \emph{cannot} disturb the
  corpus's far-pair (dissimilarity) structure, by construction rather than
  by tuning. The test suite enforces it (a near pair receives more than
  $10^3$ times the gradient of an orthogonal pair).
  \item \textbf{Term by term:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\partial \Phi(-r/2\sigma) / \partial r$ --- how fast the flip
    probability changes as the pair distance $r$ changes: exactly the
    signal backpropagation sends to a pair during training.
    \item $\propto$ --- proportional to: equal up to a constant that does
    not involve $r$.
    \item $e^{-r^2/8\sigma^2}$ --- a Gaussian in $r$: near $1$ for close
    pairs, and collapsing extremely fast once $r$ exceeds a few multiples
    of $\sigma$ --- at $r = 3\sigma$ it is already below $e^{-9/8} \approx
    0.32$ of its peak and falling as the \emph{square} of the distance in
    the exponent.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Neighbor overlap at $k$.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What it says:} For each item, the fraction of its $k$
  nearest neighbors that two embeddings (base and tuned) have in common;
  the report uses $k = 10$ and averages over held-out items.
  \item \textbf{Why it matters:} It is the retrieval-relevant measure of
  ``did the model change who is similar to whom'' --- comparing neighbor
  \emph{identities}, which global similarity statistics can miss --- and it
  is the loop's licensing criterion ($\ge 0.90$).
  \item \textbf{Term by term:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $k$ --- the neighborhood size: how many nearest neighbors each
    item's list contains ($10$ here, matching a retrieval page).
    \item overlap for one item --- $|A \cap B| / k$, where $A$ and $B$ are
    the item's neighbor sets under the two embeddings, $\cap$ is the set
    intersection (members common to both), and $|\cdot|$ counts members:
    $1$ means identical neighborhoods, $0$ fully reshuffled.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Significance machinery of the loop section.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{McNemar's exact test} asks whether a model change helped or
  hurt on \emph{paired} outcomes: it looks only at the discordant queries
  (helped by one model, failed by the other --- here $118$ against $131$)
  and asks whether that split is farther from $50{:}50$ than coin flips
  would produce.
  \item \textbf{The paired sign test} does the same for per-item
  quantities: of $408$ held-out flagged items, $406$ improved --- the
  probability of a split that lopsided under ``no effect'' is
  astronomically small ($p \approx 10^{-118}$), which is how a tiny median
  effect can still be unambiguous.
  \item \textbf{Bootstrap confidence intervals} quantify uncertainty
  without distributional assumptions: resample the data with replacement
  many times, recompute the statistic each time, and read the spread; an
  interval containing zero means the sign of the effect is not established.
  \item \textbf{AUC} (area under the ROC curve, equivalently the
  Mann--Whitney statistic) measures a predictor's ability to rank: the
  probability that a randomly chosen failing document scores higher on the
  predictor than a randomly chosen non-failing one --- $0.5$ is chance,
  $1.0$ is perfect ordering.
\end{itemize}

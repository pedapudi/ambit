\begin{abstract}
Embedding-based systems --- retrieval, retrieval-augmented generation,
deduplication, clustering --- depend on a single geometric property: items that
must be distinguished must remain distinguishable under the similarity metric.
We call the failure of this property \emph{crowding}, and we argue it is the
correct diagnostic target for embedded corpora: crowding is a loss of
\emph{resolution}, it is predominantly \emph{local}, and it hides from the
global averages (mean pairwise cosine, isotropy scores, effective rank) that
practitioners routinely consult. This report describes \textsf{ambit}, an
open-source instrument that measures how a corpus occupies its embedding
space. The design is organized around three commitments. First, occupancy is
measured \emph{continuously over all scales}: the primary object is the
empirical pair-distance distribution, read exactly (no histogram bins, grid
cells, or fixed neighbor counts, whose size and placement provably change the
answer), against analytic or cheaply simulated null models. Second, every
statistic is \emph{null-calibrated}: values are reported as deviations from a
matched well-spread reference, including an anisotropy-conditioned reference
that separates the ubiquitous ``cone effect'' from clustering the cone cannot
explain. Third, diagnosis descends to \emph{named entities}: per-item scores
(a distance-to-measure field with Wasserstein-stability guarantees; expected
retrieval collisions under an exact Gaussian query-noise law) replace per-cell
counts, which cannot identify the items at risk. We derive an operational
scalar, the \emph{resolution bandwidth} $\sstar$ --- the largest query-noise
scale at which the expected number of retrieval collisions per item stays
below one --- prove its conservative (union-bound) semantics, and show that
the widely used uniformity loss of Wang and Isola is the logarithm of a
Chernoff bound on the same quantity, endowing its temperature parameter with
units. Controlled experiments quantify the fragility of binned occupancy (a
half-cell lattice shift moves a hexbin peak count by $30\%$ and relocates the
peak on fixed data), verify the confusion law to Monte-Carlo precision
(maximum deviation $9{\times}10^{-4}$ over a $6{\times}4$ grid of angles and
noise scales), and trace a planted $5\%$ near-duplicate pocket through every
layer of the instrument: liftoff of the crowding curve at $\cos\approx0.94$,
an occupancy-discrepancy $z$ of $-55$, a $3.7\times$ collapse of the
per-item field's low tail, exact recovery of the pocket's cardinality and
scales by the merge tree, and a $26\%$ loss of noise budget
($\sstar\!: 0.203 \to 0.150$). We also report honest negative results,
including a measured near-redundancy between the confusion functional and
uniformity ($r = 0.978$) whose single order-flip is nonetheless operationally
informative. A case study on a public million-document legal corpus and a
measurement-driven training recipe with a gradient-locality guarantee
complete the report. \textsf{ambit} is unsupervised by design --- no labels,
gold pairs, or ground truth --- and its core depends only on \textsf{numpy}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =========================================================================

An embedding model maps items to vectors so that semantic relatedness becomes
geometric proximity. Once a corpus is embedded --- typically onto the unit
sphere $\sphere$, with similarity the cosine of the angle between items ---
every downstream capability reduces to one primitive: \emph{given a direction,
list the items near it}. The primitive degrades in a specific way when
distinct items land too close together: queries cannot separate them, and the
system exhibits impostors at the top of rankings, near-duplicates that cannot
be ordered, and clusters that blur into one another. We refer to this failure
mode as \emph{crowding} and to its complement --- the corpus's ability to
keep distinct items distinct --- as \emph{resolution}.

Crowding is a difficult measurement target for two reasons, developed
gradually in \S\ref{sec:geometry}: it is \emph{local} (a collapsing pocket
barely moves any global average), and the standard instruments for localizing
density --- histograms, hexbin lattices, voxel grids, fixed-$k$ neighbor
counts --- carry hidden discretization parameters whose values change the
answer (\S\ref{sec:binned}). This report describes \textsf{ambit}, an
instrument built to measure occupancy and crowding without either failure.
Its contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A first-principles account of why crowding hides}
  (\S\ref{sec:geometry}): items as directions, retrieval neighborhoods as
  spherical caps, the concentration of measure that empties the null's tail,
  and the arithmetic by which a pathological pocket vanishes into a mean.
\item \textbf{A quantitative critique of binned occupancy}
  (\S\ref{sec:binned}), with controlled experiments on the tool's own binned
  displays: headline lattice statistics are substantially properties of the
  lattice, not the data.
\item \textbf{A continuous, null-calibrated measurement layer}
  (\S\ref{sec:continuous}): the exact pair-closeness curve against a uniform
  and an anisotropy-conditioned null; a one-number occupancy discrepancy via
  Stolarsky's invariance principle; a per-item distance-to-measure field with
  a stability guarantee; and a threshold-free merge tree. Every component is
  exercised on a single traced benchmark --- a planted $5\%$ near-duplicate
  pocket --- so the layers can be read against one another.
\item \textbf{Operational semantics} (\S\ref{sec:semantics}): an exact
  Gaussian confusion law, verified to Monte-Carlo precision; the resolution
  bandwidth $\sstar$; a Chernoff identity giving the Wang--Isola uniformity
  functional operational units; a precise scope statement; and two negative
  results recorded so they stay dead.
\item \textbf{An open instrument and a training recipe}
  (\S\ref{sec:instrument}--\S\ref{sec:training}): a streaming numpy-only
  core, a narrative report, a public million-document case study, and
  measurement-driven regularizers with a gradient-locality guarantee.
\end{enumerate}

Throughout, \textsf{ambit} is \emph{unsupervised by design}: no labels, gold
pairs, or relevance judgments are consumed. We are explicit about the nature
of the contribution: the mathematics assembled here is, with small exceptions,
classical. The contribution is an \emph{instrument} --- the selection,
calibration, and composition of known functionals into a measurement system
with stated guarantees, stated nulls, and stated scope --- together with the
negative results that pruned its design space.

\section{The Geometry of Crowding, From First Principles}
\label{sec:geometry}
% =========================================================================

This section builds the intuition the rest of the report formalizes. Nothing
in it is difficult; the point is that three elementary observations, taken
together, dictate the entire measurement design.

\subsection{Items are directions; retrieval neighborhoods are caps}

Cosine-compared embeddings live on the unit sphere: each item is a
\emph{direction}, and similarity is the angle between directions. A retrieval
neighborhood --- ``everything scoring above $0.8$ against this query'' --- is
a \emph{spherical cap}: the disc of directions within a fixed angle of the
query (Figure~\ref{fig:cap}). This picture converts every occupancy question
into a cap question. Where does the corpus concentrate? --- which caps hold
more items than their share of the sphere's area. How crowded is an item? ---
how small a cap around it already contains other items. What will retrieval
confuse? --- items sharing a small cap are \emph{future collisions}: a query
landing near one cannot avoid the others.

\subsection{In high dimension, ``random'' means ``orthogonal''}

Fix one item on $\sphere$. Almost all of a high-dimensional sphere's area lies
in a thin band $90^\circ$ from it, so a random direction is almost surely
near-orthogonal to it: the pair cosine of independent uniform directions
concentrates as $\mathcal N(0, 1/d)$. At $d = 768$ the standard deviation is
$1/\sqrt{768} = 0.0361$; our measured value on $4{,}000$ sampled uniform
points is $0.0361$ (Figure~\ref{fig:conc}). Three consequences follow.
First, ``similar'' is a tiny window: $\cos = 0.3$ is already an
$8\sigma$ event for unrelated items. Second, the well-spread null's
small-distance tail is \emph{empty} --- in $19$ of $19$ simulated uniform
corpora of this size, the fraction of pairs with $\cos \ge 0.30$ is exactly
zero --- so \emph{any} observed mass there is unambiguous structure. Distance
concentration, the classical curse of high dimension \cite{beyer1999}, is for
a null-calibrated instrument a blessing: every close pair is a finding.
Third, and less happily: because almost every pair is a near-orthogonal
background pair, \emph{averages over pairs are owned by the background} ---
the observation the next subsection makes precise.

\subsection{Why averages cannot see crowding}
\label{sec:averages}

Consider the benchmark corpus used throughout this report: $n = 4{,}000$
items at $d = 768$, uniform except for a planted pocket of $k = 200$
near-duplicates (median intra-pocket cosine $0.929$) --- five percent of the
corpus rendered mutually interchangeable, a catastrophic defect for any
retrieval system built on it. The pocket contributes
$\binom{200}{2} / \binom{4000}{2} \approx 2.5{\times}10^{-3}$ of all pairs.
The mean pairwise cosine therefore moves by about
$2.5{\times}10^{-3} \times 0.93 \approx +0.002$: from $0.000$ to $0.002$,
a shift of $6\%$ of one null standard deviation \emph{per pair} ---
invisible without a calibrated test, and utterly uninformative about
\emph{which} items failed (Figure~\ref{fig:hide}). Covariance-spectrum
summaries (effective rank, isotropy scores) are blind for the same
arithmetic reason: they are sums over the corpus, owned by the diffuse
majority. The lesson generalizes: \emph{global averages answer ``is the
whole cloud degenerate?''; crowding asks ``which part of the cloud has
collapsed, at what scale, and who is in it?''} A sound instrument must
therefore (i) read \emph{distributions}, not means; (ii) read them
\emph{at every scale}, since pockets live at small scales the bulk never
visits; (iii) \emph{calibrate} against a matched null, since absolute cosine
is not self-interpreting; and (iv) descend to \emph{named items}, since the
actionable output is a list of ids. These four desiderata are the design of
\S\ref{sec:continuous}.

\section{Related Work}
% =========================================================================

\paragraph{Anisotropy and resolution.}
That learned representations occupy a narrow cone was established by Gao et
al.\ \cite{gao2019} and Ethayarajh \cite{ethayarajh2019}; Mu et al.\
\cite{mu2018} showed that removing the mean and a few dominant directions
restores much of the usable geometry, and Cai et al.\ \cite{cai2021}
exhibited local manifold structure inside the cone. Global isotropy summaries
include effective rank \cite{roy2007} and IsoScore \cite{rudman2022}. Wang
and Isola \cite{wang2020} decompose contrastive representation quality into
alignment and uniformity; the uniformity functional recurs in
\S\ref{sec:semantics} with new units. Hubness is a high-dimensional pathology
of the metric itself \cite{radovanovic2010}.

\paragraph{Embedding-quality scores.}
A family of label-free scores summarizes the covariance or Gram spectrum:
RankMe \cite{garrido2023}, $\alpha$-ReQ \cite{agrawal2022}, and relatives
(surveyed in \cite{tsitsulin2023}). A structural fact governs the family:
with a linear kernel on unit vectors the nonzero Gram spectrum equals the
uncentered second-moment spectrum, so such scores re-parameterize one
another; genuinely new signal requires a nonlinear kernel --- the Vendi score
\cite{friedman2023,pasarkar2024}, i.e.\ effective mode counts from
kernel-spectrum entropy \cite{giraldo2015,ospanov2024} --- or a different
object altogether. \textsf{ambit} adopts this dichotomy as a design filter
(\S\ref{sec:filters}).

\paragraph{Spatial statistics.}
Ripley's $K$ function \cite{ripley1976,besag1977} reads clustering as a
continuous function of scale from the interpoint-distance distribution; on
the sphere the null is a cap-area law and, the domain being boundaryless,
planar edge corrections vanish \cite{robeson2014}; global envelopes make
whole-curve inference honest \cite{myllymaki2017}. The modifiable areal unit
problem \cite{gehlke1934,openshaw1979,openshaw1984} and the ecological
fallacy \cite{robinson1950} are the canonical statements of why
partition-based statistics are fragile and non-attributive; histogram
inefficiency and the origin problem are classical
\cite{scott1979,freedman1981,silverman1986}, with the averaged shifted
histogram \cite{scott1985} the placement-free repair. Stolarsky's invariance
principle \cite{stolarsky1973,brauchart2011} identifies the mean pairwise
chord with the all-caps-all-scales $L_2$ discrepancy.

\paragraph{Geometric inference.}
The distance to a measure \cite{chazal2011,chazalmichel2021} replaces kernel
density estimates and $k$th-neighbor distances with a quantile-averaged
distance function, uniformly Lipschitz in the Wasserstein metric of the
input. Mode structure without thresholds is superlevel-set ($H_0$)
persistence: ToMATo \cite{chazal2013} and HDBSCAN's condensed tree with
excess-of-mass selection \cite{campello2015} are two normalizations of the
same object \cite{rolle2020}, resting on Hartigan's cluster tree
\cite{hartigan1981,chaudhuri2010} with stability from persistence theory
\cite{cohensteiner2007}.

\paragraph{Projections and density.}
Neighborhood-preserving layouts do not preserve density
\cite{wattenberg2016,narayan2021}, and ambient kernel density estimation
converges at $O(n^{-4/(4+d)})$ --- vacuous at $d$ in the hundreds
\cite{silverman1986}. Local intrinsic dimension estimators
\cite{levina2005,facco2017} are used only per-item and comparatively.

Figure~\ref{fig:maup} and Table~\ref{tab:maup} show the two MAUP effects
\cite{openshaw1984} on the tool's own display: \emph{zonation} (placement: a
half-cell shift moves the headline peak count from $310$ to $403$ and
relocates the peak cell) and \emph{scale} (a factor $2.4$ across reasonable
column counts). Neither perturbation touches the data. The classical
demonstration that re-zoning fixed data can move a correlation across
essentially $[-1,+1]$ \cite{openshaw1979} is the extreme form of the same
phenomenon. Histograms additionally pay an efficiency tax for
discretization --- MISE $O(n^{-2/3})$ against $O(n^{-4/5})$ for kernel
estimators \cite{scott1979,freedman1981} --- and averaging over placements
(the ASH \cite{scott1985}) recovers both placement-independence and the
kernel rate. \textsf{ambit} retains one hexbin \emph{display}, de-fragilized
exactly this way, captioned as a view, not a measurement.

\subsection{One hidden scale each; duplicate blindness}

Crowding is multi-scale: near-duplicate pockets live at tiny angles, topical
clumps at moderate ones, the anisotropy cone at large ones. Every bin width,
shell count, and fixed neighbor count $k$ silently commits to one scale.
Injecting $200$ near-identical points inside an already-dense cell moves the
hexbin peak from $340$ to $540$ --- indistinguishable from a benign hot cell
--- while the continuous per-item field of \S\ref{sec:dtm} collapses by a
factor of $18$ at its first percentile. A fixed $k$ in a kNN graph is the
same defect in rank clothing: in a dense pocket $k{=}10$ spans a tiny radius,
in a sparse rind a huge one, so ``neighborhood'' denotes a different physical
scale at every point --- precisely what a crowding measure must not let
drift.

\subsection{Steps, nulls, names, and projections}

Four further costs are structural. \emph{Instability:} a bin count is a step
function of the data with no perturbation guarantee; the field of
\S\ref{sec:dtm} is provably Lipschitz in the Wasserstein distance of the
input and measured stable (self-correlation $0.997$ under jitter at
$d{=}768$). \emph{No null:} a peak count carries no statement of what a
well-spread corpus would show --- a perfectly uniform cloud still has a
hottest cell. \emph{No names} (the ecological fallacy \cite{robinson1950}):
a cell count cannot say which items are crowded; the actionable output of a
diagnosis is a list of ids. \emph{Projection:} binning a 2-D layout measures
the shadow --- far-apart groups collide in a cell, orthogonal crowding
vanishes, and nonlinear layouts actively destroy density
\cite{wattenberg2016}. The repair is not a better ambient density estimate
(kernel density estimation is hopeless at $d$ in the hundreds
\cite{silverman1986}) but the abandonment of partitions for scale-free
functionals of interpoint distances, whose guarantees carry no dimension
factor.

\section{Continuous, Null-Calibrated Occupancy}
\label{sec:continuous}
% =========================================================================

Let $x_1,\dots,x_n \in \sphere$ (unit-normalized; cosine
$c_{ij} = \langle x_i,x_j\rangle$; chord $r_{ij} = \lVert x_i - x_j\rVert =
\sqrt{2 - 2 c_{ij}}$). \textsf{ambit} computes exact full-corpus second
moments by streaming and draws a bounded uniform reservoir plus a large
random-pair cosine sample ($2{\times}10^5$ pairs by default) for everything
else. Throughout this section the running benchmark of
\S\ref{sec:averages} --- uniform $n{=}4{,}000$, $d{=}768$, with and without
the planted $200$-item pocket --- traces every layer.

\subsection{The crowding curve}
\label{sec:kcurve}

\begin{definition}[Pair-closeness curve]
$K(t) = \Pr_{(i,j)}[\, c_{ij} \ge t \,]$: the fraction of distinct pairs at
least $t$-similar, estimated \emph{exactly} from the pair sample by sorting
--- no bins, no origin, no width.
\end{definition}

$K$ is Ripley's $K$ function on the sphere \cite{ripley1976} in cosine
coordinates. Two references with matched sample size accompany it
(Figure~\ref{fig:kcurve}):

\emph{(i) The uniform null.} For i.i.d.\ uniform points the pair cosine
satisfies $c = 2B - 1$, $B \sim
\mathrm{Beta}\!\big(\tfrac{d-1}{2},\tfrac{d-1}{2}\big)$, so null pair samples
are drawn directly --- no $d$-dimensional vectors are materialized. The
sphere being boundaryless, no edge correction exists to get wrong
\cite{robeson2014}. A pointwise envelope over $19$ replicates is a display
band; whole-curve inference, when wanted, is a rank envelope
\cite{myllymaki2017}.

\emph{(ii) The anisotropy-conditioned null.} Real corpora are cone-shaped for
benign reasons; judging them against uniformity alone over-alarms. The second
reference is the \emph{angular central Gaussian} with the corpus's own
covariance spectrum --- $x = \Sigma^{1/2} g / \lVert \Sigma^{1/2} g\rVert$,
sampled from the eigenvalues alone by rotation invariance: the corpus's
second-moment cone \emph{without} its clustering. Height of the data curve
above this reference is crowding the cone cannot explain.

On the benchmark, the data curve exceeds the uniform envelope from
$\cos \approx 0.94$ --- the pocket announces itself at precisely the
near-duplicate scale --- and carries a shoulder of excess mass
($K \approx 2.4{\times}10^{-3}$, matching the pocket's pair share) that the
ACG reference, built from the same corpus's covariance, does not reproduce:
the crowding is clustering, not cone.

\subsection{The occupancy discrepancy (Stolarsky $z$)}

\begin{proposition}[Stolarsky \cite{stolarsky1973}]
For points on $\sphere$, the spherical-cap $L_2$ discrepancy --- the squared
deviation between empirical and expected cap occupancy, integrated over
\emph{all} cap centers and \emph{all} cap radii --- equals, up to explicit
constants, a constant minus the mean pairwise chord
$\frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{i,j}\lVert x_i - x_j\rVert$.
\end{proposition}

The mean pairwise chord is therefore ``every lattice at every scale at
once'': the exact completion of what binned occupancy approximates, with no
lattice to choose. \textsf{ambit} reports it as a $z$-score against the
uniform null at matched pair-sample size, pricing in sampling noise.
Measured on the benchmark: the uniform corpus scores $z = +1.5$ (consistent
with its null); planting the $5\%$ pocket moves the mean chord from
$1.41405$ to $1.41160$ --- a shift of $0.17\%$ --- which the razor-thin null
resolves as $z = -55$. Tightening the pocket further (members within a cap
of angular radius $\approx 0.15$) drives the same statistic to $z = -430$:
detection strength scales with the pathology while the uniform control stays
at noise level.

\begin{definition}[Empirical DTM \cite{chazal2011,chazalmichel2021}]
For mass fraction $m$ and $k = \lceil m n\rceil$:
$\dtm_m(x_i) = \big(\tfrac1k \sum_{j=1}^{k} r_{i,(j)}^2\big)^{1/2}$, with $r_{i,(j)}$ the
distance to $x_i$'s $j$th nearest reservoir point --- the radius the item
needs to gather an $m$-share of the corpus.
\end{definition}

Small radius means crowded; large, isolated (Figure~\ref{fig:dtm}). The one
knob $m$ is a \emph{mass fraction} --- scale-free in $n$ --- and replaces
bin width, grid origin, and fixed $k$ simultaneously. The estimator inherits
the stability theorem $\lVert \dtm^P_m - \dtm^Q_m\rVert_\infty \le
m^{-1/2}\, W_2(P, Q)$ \cite{chazal2011}: a small perturbation of the corpus
provably moves every item's score a small amount --- the guarantee step
functions lack, and measured: jittering every coordinate by $10^{-3}$ leaves
the field $0.997$-correlated with itself. Both tails are actionable and
\emph{named}: the low tail is the crowded-item list (each entry annotated
with its expected collision count at the corpus's own $\sstar$;
\S\ref{sec:semantics}), the high tail the coverage voids. In strongly
cone-shaped corpora the entire field sits below the uniform band --- the cone
shortens every radius --- and the instrument says so rather than reporting a
meaningless all-items count; the finding in that regime is the shape of the
low tail against the corpus's own bulk.

Between the global curve and the per-item field sits \emph{structure}: one
broad pile, or many tight pockets, and who constitutes each?
\textsf{ambit} surfaces the hierarchy its clustering backend already
computes and previously flattened: single linkage on the mutual-reachability
metric \cite{campello2015}, condensed at a minimum pocket size, selected by
excess of mass in $\lambda = 1/\mathrm{distance}$ --- equivalently $H_0$
persistence with the elder rule \cite{chazal2013,rolle2020}. Each selected
pocket reports birth, death, prominence, size, and member ids
(Figure~\ref{fig:tree}). Two implementation findings mattered. First, the
naive elder rule is wrong for this purpose: the tightest pocket is the
\emph{eldest} component and survives to the root, so the bulk ``dies into
it''; excess-of-mass selection resolves the inversion. Second,
single-linkage chains inside a tight pocket are caterpillars, so membership
is reported as the condensed core plus side branches that fell out in the
tight regime (below the geometric mean of birth and death), excluding bulk
stragglers. On the benchmark the tree recovers the planted pocket exactly
(size $200$, birth $0.063$, death $0.898$, prominence $0.835$); on a
two-pocket variant ($60$ and $40$ planted members at $d = 64$) it recovers
sizes $60$ and $40$ exactly.

\subsection{Complexity, and the design filters}
\label{sec:filters}

All continuous-layer statistics run on the reservoir or the pair sample: the
curve and its nulls are $O(P \log P)$ in the pair-sample size; the DTM field
is a blocked Gram top-$k$, $O(n_r^2 d)$ on a capped subsample (seconds on
CPU); the merge tree is a dense Prim MST on $n_r \le 4{,}096$ rows plus a
linear condensation pass. Full-corpus second moments stream in one pass.

Candidate metrics were admitted by five filters, recorded because they prune
future proposals cheaply: \emph{spectrum} (does it reduce, under a linear
kernel, to the covariance spectrum already reported?), \emph{continuity} (a
continuous functional, or a step function of bin/rank/partition
membership?), \emph{null} (does it come with a calibrated reference?),
\emph{naming} (can it score items, or only regions?), and
\emph{supervision} (label-free?). These excluded, among others,
RankMe, NESum, and stable rank (spectrum), $k$-means-conditioned statistics
(continuity), raw counts (null), all cell aggregates (naming), and
neural-collapse metrics \cite{papyan2020} (supervision).

\section{Operational Semantics: the Resolution Bandwidth}
\label{sec:semantics}
% =========================================================================

Geometric functionals ask ``how uniform is the sphere?''; a practitioner
asks ``how much query sloppiness can this corpus absorb?''. This section
converts the former into the latter exactly, then measures the conversion.

\subsection{An exact pairwise confusion law}

Model a query aimed at item $x$ as $q = x + \sigma g$, $g \sim \mathcal
N(0, I_d)$ --- the simplest honest model of ``the query means $x$ but lands
nearby.'' Retrieval errs on competitor $y$ when $\langle q, y\rangle >
\langle q, x\rangle$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact flip law]\label{prop:flip}
For unit vectors at chord distance $r = \lVert x - y \rVert$,
$\;\Pr[\langle q, y\rangle > \langle q, x\rangle] =
\Phi\!\big({-r}/{2\sigma}\big)$, exactly, in any dimension.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\langle q, y - x\rangle$ is Gaussian with mean $\langle x, y - x\rangle =
\cos\theta - 1 = -r^2/2$ and standard deviation $\sigma \lVert y - x\rVert =
\sigma r$; the flip probability is $\Phi\big((-r^2/2)/(\sigma r)\big)$.
Geometrically (Figure~\ref{fig:bisector}): the boundary is the bisector at
distance $r/2$, crossed with probability $\Phi(-(r/2)/\sigma)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{$C(\sigma)$, $\sstar$, and the uniformity bound}

Summing the law over competitors gives the expected number of items
outranking the intended target,
\[
C(\sigma) = \sum_{j \ne i} \Phi\!\left(-\frac{r_{ij}}{2\sigma}\right)
\;\approx\; (n{-}1)\!\int \Phi\!\left(-\frac{r}{2\sigma}\right) dK(r),
\]
a functional of the pair-distance distribution alone. By Boole's inequality
the sum bounds the probability of \emph{any} error under arbitrary
competitor correlations, so $C$ is conservative: hubness makes it loose,
never wrong.

\begin{definition}[Resolution bandwidth]
$\sstar$ is the largest $\sigma$ with $C(\sigma) \le 1$: the most noise
queries can carry before, in expectation, one wrong item outranks the right
one. $C$ is monotone; bisection inverts it.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Uniformity as a Chernoff bound]\label{prop:chernoff}
With $U_t = \log \E\, e^{-t\lVert x - y\rVert^2}$ the uniformity functional
of \cite{wang2020}: $\;C(\sigma) \le (n{-}1)\, e^{U_t}$ at
$t = 1/(8\sigma^2)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\Phi(-z) \le e^{-z^2/2}$ with $z = r/2\sigma$.
\end{proof}

The widely used uniformity temperature is thus not arbitrary: $t = 2$ is a
collision bound at query noise $\sigma = 0.25$. \textsf{ambit} reports
uniformity with this reading attached.

\subsection{Scope}

The semantics carry their boundary: (i) the corpus is treated as its own
query population --- exact for deduplication, clustering, and
corpus-as-queries retrieval; extrapolation to an external workload assumes
the workload lands where the documents are; (ii) the noise channel is
isotropic, while real query drift is structured; (iii) the aggregate is an
upper bound. $\sstar$ is therefore a conservative intra-corpus guarantee,
strongest used comparatively --- one corpus embedded two ways, or one
embedding over time.

\section{The Instrument}
\label{sec:instrument}
% =========================================================================

\subsection{Architecture}

\textsf{ambit} is a library with a thin CLI. One canonical in-memory type
(\texttt{EmbeddingSet}) flows through a pipeline: a \emph{streaming scan}
(one pass over \texttt{.npy/.npz/.parquet/.csv/.jsonl}, single file or
sharded directory) accumulates exact second moments, norms, and a uniform
reservoir; a context builder derives projections (PCA by default), the
eigenspectrum, a kNN graph (optionally filtered to \emph{reciprocal}
neighbors, which suppresses hubs by construction), unsupervised clusters,
and the pair-cosine sample; a figure registry renders a single
self-contained, theme-adaptive HTML file with no external requests. The core
depends on \textsf{numpy} alone; heavier capabilities (tabular IO, ANN
graphs, UMAP, GPU paths, on-the-fly embedding via an OpenAI-compatible
endpoint, the training extra) are optional and degrade gracefully. A
comparison mode aligns two embeddings of the same items by id and prefixes
the report with a local neighbor-overlap view read against global
representational-similarity statistics (CKA \cite{kornblith2019}, kernel and
rigid distances); the local/global pair can disagree informatively --- a
high-CKA, low-overlap outcome is a re-skin that preserves gross structure
while rewriting fine neighborhoods.

\subsection{Report design}

Three principles govern the report. \emph{Direction, not hue}: meaning lives
in positions along annotated axes. \emph{Reference, not threshold}: every
panel draws its null. \emph{Display versus measurement}: projections lay
figures out but never carry the statistic. The figures are ordered as a
narrative --- see it; how crowded, at what scale; who is affected; what
structure it forms; why the space behaves so --- and every card carries an
in-place explainer with an intuition diagram and the card's unstated context
(null construction, subsample sizes, id provenance). One legibility finding
is recorded: a minimum-spanning-tree view drawn over the projection --- even
restricted to tighter-than-bulk bridges --- remained too busy to read on
real corpora and was demoted to a hidden option; the condensed tree carries
the same object legibly as prominence bars.

\section{From Measurement to Gradient}
\label{sec:training}
% =========================================================================

The confusion kernel is differentiable, so the measurement layer doubles as
a training-time regularizer with a stated guarantee.

\subsection{Losses and the gradient-locality guarantee}

For a batch $z_1,\dots,z_B$ (normalized) and a \emph{measured} target scale
$\sigma$:
\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{conf}}
= \mathop{\mathrm{mean}}_{(i,j) \notin \mathcal E}
  \Phi\!\left(-\frac{\lVert z_i - z_j\rVert}{2\sigma}\right),
\qquad
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pres}}
= \mathop{\mathrm{mean}}_i \;
  \mathrm{KL}\!\left(p^{\mathrm{base}}_i \,\middle\|\, p_i\right),
\]
where $\mathcal E$ excludes positive and guarded pairs and $p_i$
($p^{\mathrm{base}}_i$) are temperature-softmax similarity rows under the
tuned (frozen base) model. $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{conf}}$ is the batch
estimate of $C(\sigma)$; since $\partial\Phi(-r/2\sigma)/\partial r \propto
e^{-r^2/8\sigma^2}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:gradwin}), its gradient vanishes
exponentially for pairs beyond $\approx 3\sigma$: far-pair (dissimilarity)
structure \emph{cannot} be disturbed, a property enforced in the test suite
(a near pair receives $>10^3\times$ the gradient of an orthogonal pair).
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pres}}$ is the neighbor-overlap comparison in
differentiable form --- the explicit statement of ``do not rewrite who is
similar to whom.'' (Implementation note: the chord computation floors
$2 - 2c$ before the square root; at exactly zero the root's infinite
derivative poisons masked entries with $0 \cdot \infty$ NaN gradients.)

\subsection{Mining, protocol, and measured effect}

Batch composition is driven by the measurements: sampling weights
proportional to per-item collision counts (with a uniform floor so the bulk
anchors the geometry), and negatives drafted from the \emph{confusable
window} --- between the measured liftoff and the alignment scale of true
pairs --- under a false-negative guard: an anchor's top-$m$ base-model
neighbors are never negatives, because the window is precisely where
unlabeled true relatives live. The protocol is staged: diagnose and take the
cheapest licensed fix (cone $\Rightarrow$ linear post-processing; duplicates
$\Rightarrow$ data repair; only genuine beyond-cone clumping licenses
training); train with $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{task}} + \lambda_c \mathcal
L_{\mathrm{conf}} + \lambda_p \mathcal L_{\mathrm{pres}}$ at the measured
$\sigma$; verify on a held-out reservoir never seen by mining, and never
grade with the functional that was optimized.

% =========================================================================
\section{Limitations and Future Work}
% =========================================================================

\textbf{External validation.} The instrument's readouts are geometrically
grounded and internally verified; the outstanding scientific step is a study
correlating them with measured retrieval outcomes across corpora and
encoders, using relevance judgments only to \emph{score} the unsupervised
predictions. The confusion transform makes one falsifiable prediction its
near-redundant sibling does not (Table~\ref{tab:redundancy}): duplicate
pathology outranks diffuse collapse.
\textbf{Query distributions.} Accepting an optional \emph{unlabeled} query
set would replace the corpus-as-queries assumption with a measured cross-set
confusion, within the tool's supervision constraint.
\textbf{Sharper aggregates.} The union bound is safe but loose where hubs
live; Gaussian comparison inequalities may yield two-sided estimates.
\textbf{Recoverability.} ``Resolution headroom'' --- the fraction of
measured crowding removable by a budgeted transform class, computed rather
than predicted --- would answer the practitioner's first question (``is this
the embedding's fault or my data's?'').
\textbf{Streaming structure.} The pair curve sketches trivially; the merge
tree does not; a mergeable approximate hierarchy with prominence error
bounds would extend the structural layer past the reservoir.

% =========================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% =========================================================================

Crowding is where embedded systems quietly fail, and it is exactly the
property that global averages cannot see and binned displays cannot be
trusted to measure. The instrument described here rests on one methodological
commitment applied uniformly: \emph{read continuous objects at every scale
against stated nulls, and descend to named items}. The mathematics is
classical --- Ripley's curve, Stolarsky's identity, the distance to a
measure, the condensed cluster tree, a Gaussian tail bound --- and the
contribution is their selection, calibration, and composition into a system
with stated guarantees and stated scope, traced end to end through a single
benchmark whose planted pathology every layer detects, localizes, prices,
and (in the training layer) partially repairs. The negative results are
reported alongside the positive ones because an instrument is trustworthy
only when its designers say what it cannot do.

\begin{abstract}
Embedding-based systems --- retrieval, retrieval-augmented generation,
deduplication, clustering --- depend on a single geometric property: items that
must be distinguished must remain distinguishable under the similarity metric.
We call the failure of this property \emph{crowding}, and we argue it is the
correct diagnostic target for embedded corpora: crowding is a loss of
\emph{resolution}, it is predominantly \emph{local}, and it hides from the
global averages (mean pairwise cosine, isotropy scores, effective rank) that
practitioners routinely consult. This report describes \textsf{ambit}, an
open-source instrument that measures how a corpus occupies its embedding
space. The design is organized around three commitments. First, occupancy is
measured \emph{continuously over all scales}: the primary object is the
empirical pair-distance distribution, read exactly (no histogram bins, grid
cells, or fixed neighbor counts, whose size and placement provably change the
answer), against analytic or cheaply simulated null models. Second, every
statistic is \emph{null-calibrated}: values are reported as deviations from a
matched well-spread reference, including an anisotropy-conditioned reference
that separates the ubiquitous ``cone effect'' from clustering the cone cannot
explain. Third, diagnosis descends to \emph{named entities}: per-item scores
(a distance-to-measure field with Wasserstein-stability guarantees; expected
retrieval collisions under an exact Gaussian query-noise law) replace per-cell
counts, which cannot identify the items at risk. We derive an operational
scalar, the \emph{resolution bandwidth} $\sstar$ --- the largest query-noise
scale at which the expected number of retrieval collisions per item stays
below one --- prove its conservative (union-bound) semantics, and show that
the widely used uniformity loss of Wang and Isola is the logarithm of a
Chernoff bound on the same quantity, endowing its temperature parameter with
units. We report controlled experiments quantifying the fragility of binned
occupancy statistics (a half-cell lattice shift moves a hexbin peak count by
$30\%$ on fixed data), the sensitivity of the continuous replacements (a
$5\%$ near-duplicate pocket is a $430\sigma$ event under the mean-chord
discrepancy), and honest negative results, including a measured near-redundancy
between the confusion functional and uniformity ($r=0.978$) whose single
order-flip is nonetheless operationally informative. A case study on a public
million-document legal corpus and a measurement-driven training recipe with a
gradient-locality guarantee complete the report. \textsf{ambit} is
unsupervised by design --- no labels, gold pairs, or ground truth --- and its
core depends only on \textsf{numpy}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =========================================================================

An embedding model maps items to vectors so that semantic relatedness becomes
geometric proximity. Once a corpus is embedded --- typically onto the unit
sphere $\sphere$, with similarity the cosine of the angle between items ---
every downstream capability reduces to one primitive: \emph{given a direction,
list the items near it}. The primitive degrades in a specific way when
distinct items land too close together: queries cannot separate them, and the
system exhibits impostors at the top of rankings, near-duplicates that cannot
be ordered, and clusters that blur into one another. We refer to this failure
mode as \emph{crowding} and to its complement --- the corpus's ability to keep
distinct items distinct --- as \emph{resolution}.

Two facts make crowding a difficult measurement target. First, \emph{crowding
is local}. Learned text embeddings are almost universally anisotropic
\cite{gao2019,ethayarajh2019,mu2018}: the entire corpus occupies a narrow cone,
inflating all pairwise similarities. This global effect is well characterized
and partially correctable by simple post-processing \cite{mu2018,cai2021}. The
operationally dangerous crowding is instead a property of sub-populations ---
a pocket of a corpus collapsing while the bulk remains healthy --- and a small
pocket barely moves any global average. A mean over pairs, or a covariance
summed over points, is owned by the diffuse majority; the global number can
read ``healthy'' while a subset is unusable, and it can never say
\emph{where}. Second, the standard instruments for localizing density ---
histograms, hexbin lattices, voxel grids, fixed-$k$ neighbor counts --- carry
hidden discretization parameters whose values change the answer. Section~3
demonstrates this fragility quantitatively; it is the spatial-statistics
\emph{modifiable areal unit problem} \cite{gehlke1934,openshaw1979} imported
into embedding diagnostics.

This report describes \textsf{ambit}, an instrument built to measure occupancy
and crowding without either failure. Its contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A critique of binned occupancy} (\S3). We show, with controlled
  experiments on the tool's own binned displays, that headline statistics of
  lattice-based occupancy are substantially properties of the lattice: a
  half-cell shift of a hexbin lattice moves the peak-cell count from $310$ to
  $403$ on identical data; changing the column count from $20$ to $34$ changes
  it by a factor of $2.4$; and a pathological pocket of $200$ near-duplicates
  is invisible as such to the count while a continuous per-item field responds
  by a factor of $18$.
\item \textbf{A continuous, null-calibrated measurement layer} (\S4). The
  empirical pair-distance distribution is promoted to the central object; its
  exceedance curve is Ripley's $K$ on the sphere \cite{ripley1976}, computed
  exactly with no bins, with an analytic uniform null (whose pair cosines are
  sampled directly through a Beta transform, materializing no vectors) and an
  \emph{anisotropy-conditioned} null (an angular central Gaussian matched to
  the corpus covariance spectrum) that isolates crowding the cone cannot
  explain. Stolarsky's invariance principle \cite{stolarsky1973,brauchart2011}
  supplies a one-number summary --- the mean pairwise chord distance equals,
  up to constants, the spherical-cap $L_2$ discrepancy integrated over
  \emph{all} cap centers and radii --- which we report as a $z$-score against
  a sample-size-matched null. Localization is provided by a
  distance-to-measure field \cite{chazal2011} with its Wasserstein-Lipschitz
  stability guarantee, and structure by the condensed merge tree with
  excess-of-mass selection \cite{campello2015,chazal2013}.
\item \textbf{Operational semantics for crowding} (\S5). Under a Gaussian
  query-noise channel $q = x + \sigma g$, the probability that a competitor
  at chord distance $r$ outranks the intended target is \emph{exactly}
  $\Phi(-r/2\sigma)$, in any dimension. Summed over competitors this yields a
  conservative (union-bound) expected-collision count $C(\sigma)$, whose
  inversion at tolerance one defines the \emph{resolution bandwidth}
  $\sstar$. A two-line Chernoff argument shows the Wang--Isola uniformity
  functional \cite{wang2020} is the log of an upper bound on the same
  quantity at $t = 1/(8\sigma^2)$: an aesthetic loss acquires operational
  units. We state the scope of these semantics precisely (intra-corpus
  confusability under isotropic noise) and report the measured
  near-redundancy of $C(\sigma)$ with uniformity, including the one
  informative disagreement.
\item \textbf{A measurement-driven training recipe} (\S7). The confusion
  kernel is differentiable; \textsf{ambit} ships it as a regularizer whose
  gradient provably vanishes exponentially outside a measured
  \emph{confusable window} (so far-pair structure cannot be disturbed),
  together with a local-structure preservation term (a differentiable form of
  the neighbor-overlap comparison), collision-count-weighted batch sampling,
  and window-mining with a false-negative guard. A staged protocol ---
  diagnose, choose the cheapest licensed fix, train at the measured scale,
  verify on held-out data never seen by mining --- is demonstrated end to end
  with honestly reported effect sizes.
\item \textbf{An open instrument} (\S6, \S8). A streaming, numpy-only core
  scans corpora of $10^6$ rows; a bounded reservoir feeds a figure registry
  that renders a single self-contained HTML report, ordered as a narrative
  and annotated in place. A case study on a public million-document legal
  corpus exercises the full system.
\end{enumerate}

Throughout, \textsf{ambit} is \emph{unsupervised by design}: no labels, gold
pairs, or relevance judgments are consumed. Where user-provided labels exist
they are used only to \emph{condition} readouts (a separability panel), never
to define them.

We are explicit about the nature of the contribution: the mathematics
assembled here is, with small exceptions (\S5), classical. The contribution is
an \emph{instrument} --- the selection, calibration, and composition of known
functionals into a measurement system with stated guarantees, stated nulls,
and stated scope --- together with the negative results (\S5.4) that pruned
its design space.

\section{Background and Related Work}
% =========================================================================

\paragraph{Anisotropy and resolution.}
That learned representations occupy a narrow cone was established for
generative and contextual encoders by Gao et al.\ \cite{gao2019} (representation
degeneration) and Ethayarajh \cite{ethayarajh2019}; Mu et al.\ \cite{mu2018}
showed that removing the mean and a few dominant directions restores much of
the usable geometry, and Cai et al.\ \cite{cai2021} exhibited local manifold
structure inside the cone. Global isotropy summaries include the covariance
spectrum's effective rank \cite{roy2007}, the participation ratio, and
IsoScore \cite{rudman2022}. Wang and Isola \cite{wang2020} decompose
contrastive representation quality into \emph{alignment} and
\emph{uniformity}; the uniformity functional recurs in \S5 with new units.
Hubness --- the emergence of items that appear in disproportionately many
neighbor lists --- is a high-dimensional pathology of the metric itself
\cite{radovanovic2010} and is causally downstream of local intrinsic
dimension.

\paragraph{Embedding-quality scores.}
A family of label-free quality scores summarizes the covariance or Gram
spectrum: RankMe \cite{garrido2023}, $\alpha$-ReQ \cite{agrawal2022}, NESum,
and stable rank (surveyed in \cite{tsitsulin2023}). A structural fact governs
this family: with a linear (cosine) kernel on unit vectors, the nonzero Gram
spectrum equals the uncentered second-moment spectrum, so such scores are
re-parameterizations of one another. Genuinely new signal requires either a
nonlinear kernel --- the Vendi score \cite{friedman2023} and its R\'enyi
family \cite{pasarkar2024}, i.e.\ effective mode counts from kernel-spectrum
entropy \cite{giraldo2015}, with finite-sample corrections \cite{ospanov2024}
--- or a different mathematical object altogether. \textsf{ambit} adopts this
dichotomy as a design filter (\S4.6).

\paragraph{Spatial statistics.}
Ripley's $K$ function \cite{ripley1976,besag1977} reads clustering as a
continuous function of scale from the interpoint-distance distribution; on the
sphere the complete-spatial-randomness null is a cap-area law and, the domain
being boundaryless, the edge corrections that dominate planar practice vanish
\cite{robeson2014}. Global envelope tests \cite{myllymaki2017} make
whole-curve inference honest. The modifiable areal unit problem
\cite{gehlke1934,openshaw1979,openshaw1984} and the ecological fallacy
\cite{robinson1950} are the canonical statements of why partition-based
statistics are fragile and non-attributive; histogram inefficiency and the
origin problem are classical \cite{scott1979,freedman1981,silverman1986}, with
the averaged shifted histogram \cite{scott1985} as the placement-free repair.
Stolarsky's invariance principle \cite{stolarsky1973,brauchart2011} identifies
the mean pairwise chord distance with the all-caps-all-scales $L_2$
discrepancy on the sphere.

\paragraph{Geometric inference.}
The distance to a measure of Chazal, Cohen-Steiner and M\'erigot
\cite{chazal2011} replaces both kernel density estimates and $k$th-neighbor
distances with a quantile-averaged distance function that is uniformly
Lipschitz in the Wasserstein metric of the input; its empirical form is a
root-mean-square of $k$-nearest-neighbor distances \cite{chazalmichel2021}.
Mode structure without thresholds is provided by superlevel-set ($H_0$)
persistence: ToMATo \cite{chazal2013} and the condensed cluster tree with
excess-of-mass selection of HDBSCAN \cite{campello2015} are two
normalizations of the same object \cite{rolle2020}, resting on the cluster
tree of Hartigan \cite{hartigan1981} and its consistent estimation
\cite{chaudhuri2010}, with stability from persistence theory
\cite{cohensteiner2007}.

\paragraph{Distance concentration.}
In high dimension pairwise distances concentrate \cite{beyer1999}; for
nearest-neighbor methods this is a curse, but for a null-calibrated test it is
a blessing: the uniform null's pair-cosine distribution has standard deviation
$1/\sqrt{d}$, so its small-distance tail is \emph{empty}, and any observed
mass there is unambiguous structure. Projection-based density readings inherit
a second fragility: neighborhood-preserving layouts do not preserve density
\cite{wattenberg2016,narayan2021}, and ambient-dimension kernel density
estimation converges at $O(n^{-4/(4+d)})$ --- vacuous at $d$ in the hundreds
\cite{silverman1986}. Local intrinsic dimension estimators
\cite{levina2005,facco2017} are used in \textsf{ambit} only per-item and only
comparatively, consistent with their measured global insensitivity to small
pockets (\S3.3).

\section{The Case Against Binned Occupancy}
% =========================================================================

\textsf{ambit}'s early density views were conventional: hexbin lattices over a
2-D projection, voxel grids, radial shells, fixed-$k$ neighbor counts. This
section records why the measurement layer abandoned them. All experiments are
reproducible from the descriptions here; the first is run against the tool's
own hexbin assignment code.

\subsection{The answer belongs to the lattice}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Lattice fragility on fixed data ($n{=}4{,}000$ synthetic 2-D points,
three Gaussian clumps plus background; the tool's own hexbin assignment).}
\label{tab:maup}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
perturbation of the \emph{instrument} & peak-cell count & occupied cells\\
\midrule
lattice as placed & 310 & 256\\

\midrule

\end{table}

Table~\ref{tab:maup} shows the two MAUP effects \cite{openshaw1984} on the
tool's own display: the \emph{zonation} effect (placement: a half-cell shift
moves the headline peak count by $30\%$, and relocates which cell is the
peak) and the \emph{scale} effect (a factor $2.4$ across reasonable column
counts). Neither perturbation touches the data. The classical demonstration
that re-zoning fixed data can move a correlation across essentially
$[-1,+1]$ \cite{openshaw1979} is the extreme form of the same phenomenon.
Histograms additionally pay an efficiency tax for discretization --- MISE
$O(n^{-2/3})$ against $O(n^{-4/5})$ for kernel estimators at optimal
bandwidths \cite{scott1979,freedman1981} --- and averaging over placements
(the ASH \cite{scott1985}) recovers both placement-independence and the
kernel rate. \textsf{ambit} retains one hexbin \emph{display}, de-fragilized
exactly this way, and captions it as a view, not a measurement.

\subsection{One hidden scale each}

Crowding is multi-scale: near-duplicate pockets live at tiny angles, topical
clumps at moderate ones, the anisotropy cone at large ones. Every bin width,
shell count, and fixed neighbor count $k$ silently commits to one scale. In
the same experiment, injecting $200$ near-identical points inside an
already-dense cell moves the hexbin peak from $340$ to $540$ --- a change
indistinguishable from a benign hot cell --- while the 1st percentile of the
continuous per-item field of \S4.4 collapses by a factor of $18$. A fixed
$k$ in a kNN graph is the same defect in rank clothing: in a dense pocket
$k{=}10$ spans a tiny radius, in a sparse rind a huge one, so ``neighborhood''
denotes a different physical scale at every point.

\subsection{Steps, nulls, and names}

Three further costs are structural. (i) \emph{Instability:} a bin count is a
step function of the data with no perturbation guarantee; the field of \S4.4
is provably Lipschitz in the Wasserstein distance of the input, and measured:
under a $0.1\%$-of-span jitter its self-correlation is $0.99999$. (ii)
\emph{No null:} a peak count carries no statement of what a well-spread corpus
would show --- a perfectly uniform cloud still has a hottest cell --- whereas
every \textsf{ambit} statistic is a deviation from a matched reference. (iii)
\emph{No names} (the ecological fallacy \cite{robinson1950}): a cell count
cannot say which items are crowded; per-item fields can, and the actionable
output of a diagnosis is a list of ids. A fourth cost is inherited when the
binning is applied to a 2-D projection: the grid then measures the shadow, in
which far-apart groups collide (false crowding) and orthogonal crowding
vanishes (missed crowding); nonlinear layouts actively destroy density
\cite{wattenberg2016}. The repair is not a better ambient density estimate ---
KDE is hopeless at $d$ in the hundreds \cite{silverman1986} --- but the
abandonment of partitions for scale-free functionals of interpoint distances,
whose guarantees carry no dimension factor.

\section{Continuous, Null-Calibrated Occupancy}
% =========================================================================

Let $x_1,\dots,x_n \in \sphere$ be the embedded corpus (unit-normalized;
cosine similarity $c_{ij} = \langle x_i,x_j\rangle$; chord distance
$r_{ij} = \lVert x_i - x_j \rVert = \sqrt{2-2c_{ij}}$). \textsf{ambit}
computes exact full-corpus second moments by streaming and draws a bounded
uniform reservoir (default $2\times10^4$ rows) plus a large random-pair
cosine sample (default $2\times10^5$ pairs) for everything else.

\subsection{The crowding curve}

\begin{definition}[Pair-closeness curve]
$K(t) \;=\; \Pr_{(i,j)}\!\left[\, c_{ij} \ge t \,\right]$, the fraction of
distinct pairs at least $t$-similar, estimated exactly from the pair sample by
sorting (no bins, no origin, no width).
\end{definition}

$K$ is Ripley's $K$ function on the sphere \cite{ripley1976} in cosine
coordinates. Two references with matched sample size accompany it:

\emph{(i) The uniform null.} For i.i.d.\ uniform points on $\sphere$ the pair
cosine has density proportional to $(1-c^2)^{(d-3)/2}$; equivalently
$c = 2B - 1$ with $B \sim \mathrm{Beta}(\frac{d-1}{2},\frac{d-1}{2})$, so
null pair samples are drawn directly, materializing no $d$-dimensional
vectors. The sphere being boundaryless, no edge correction exists to get
wrong \cite{robeson2014}. A pointwise envelope over $19$ replicates is drawn
as a display band; whole-curve inference, when wanted, is a rank envelope
\cite{myllymaki2017}.

\emph{(ii) The anisotropy-conditioned null.} Real corpora are cone-shaped for
benign reasons; judging them against uniformity alone over-alarms. The second
reference is the \emph{angular central Gaussian} with the corpus's own
covariance spectrum --- $x = \Sigma^{1/2} g / \lVert \Sigma^{1/2} g \rVert$,
sampled from the eigenvalues alone by rotation-invariance --- i.e.\ the
corpus's second-moment cone \emph{without} its clustering. Height of the data
curve above this reference is crowding the cone cannot explain; the report
annotates the \emph{liftoff} scale at which the data first exceed the uniform
envelope.

In high dimension the null curve's informative region is \emph{empty}: with
$n{=}4{,}000$ uniform points at $d{=}768$, the measured pair-cosine standard
deviation is $1/\sqrt{768} = 0.0361$ and $K(0.30) = 0$ in $19$ of $19$ null
replicates, against $2.5\times10^{-3}$ when $5\%$ of the corpus is collapsed
into a small cap. Distance concentration, the classical curse
\cite{beyer1999}, is here a blessing: every close pair is a finding.

\subsection{The occupancy discrepancy (Stolarsky $z$)}

\begin{proposition}[Stolarsky \cite{stolarsky1973}]
For points on $\sphere$, the spherical-cap $L_2$ discrepancy --- the squared
deviation between empirical and expected cap occupancy, integrated over all
cap centers and all cap radii --- equals, up to explicit constants, a constant
minus the mean pairwise chord distance $\frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{i,j}\lVert x_i -
x_j\rVert$.
\end{proposition}

The mean pairwise chord is therefore ``every lattice at every scale at
once'': the exact completion of what binned occupancy approximates, with no
lattice to choose. \textsf{ambit} reports it as a $z$-score against the
uniform null with matched pair-sample size, pricing in sampling noise. The
null is razor-thin --- measured standard deviation of the null mean chord
$7\times10^{-6}$ at $(n,d) = (4{,}000, 768)$ --- so the $5\%$-pocket corpus
above sits at $z = -430$: detection with essentially infinite signal-to-noise,
computed from a quantity the tool already samples.

\subsection{Localization: the distance-to-measure field}

\begin{definition}[Empirical DTM \cite{chazal2011,chazalmichel2021}]
For mass fraction $m$, $\;\dtm_m(x_i) = \big( \tfrac1k \sum_{j=1}^{k}
r_{i,(j)}^2 \big)^{1/2}$ with $k = \lceil m n\rceil$ and $r_{i,(j)}$ the
distance to $x_i$'s $j$th nearest reservoir point: the radius the item needs
to gather an $m$-share of the corpus.
\end{definition}

Small radius means crowded; large means isolated; the field's exact CDF is
displayed against the $1$st--$99$th percentile band of the same field on a
matched uniform corpus. The single knob $m$ is a \emph{mass fraction} ---
scale-free in $n$ --- and replaces bin width, grid origin, and fixed $k$
simultaneously. The estimator inherits the stability theorem
$\lVert \dtm^P_m - \dtm^Q_m \rVert_\infty \le m^{-1/2}\, W_2(P,Q)$
\cite{chazal2011}: a small perturbation of the corpus provably moves every
item's score a small amount --- the guarantee step-function counts lack. Both
tails are actionable and \emph{named}: the low tail is the crowded-item list
(each entry annotated with its expected collision count at the corpus's own
$\sstar$; \S5), the high tail the coverage voids. One field thus subsumes the
former hot-cell accents and void-disc grid. In strongly cone-shaped corpora
the entire field sits below the uniform band --- the cone shortens every
radius --- and the report says so rather than reporting a meaningless
all-items count; the finding in that regime is the shape of the low tail
against the corpus's own bulk.

\subsection{Structure: the condensed merge tree}

Between the global curve and the per-item field sits the question of
\emph{structure}: is crowding one broad pile or many tight pockets, and which
items constitute each? \textsf{ambit} surfaces the hierarchy its clustering
backend already computes and previously flattened: single linkage on the
mutual-reachability metric (the HDBSCAN construction \cite{campello2015}),
condensed at a minimum pocket size, with excess-of-mass selection in
$\lambda = 1/\mathrm{distance}$ --- equivalently, superlevel-set $H_0$
persistence with the elder rule \cite{chazal2013,rolle2020}. Each selected
pocket reports its \emph{birth} (the scale at which its first members hold
together), \emph{death} (merger into the bulk), \emph{prominence}
(death $-$ birth), size, and member ids; no flat threshold is chosen anywhere.
Two implementation notes mattered in practice. First, the naive elder rule is
wrong for this purpose: the tightest pocket is the \emph{eldest} component and
survives to the root, so the bulk ``dies into it''; excess-of-mass selection
resolves this correctly. Second, single-linkage chains inside a tight pocket
are caterpillars, so a pocket's membership is reported as its condensed core
plus side branches that fell out in the tight regime (below the geometric mean
of birth and death), which excludes bulk stragglers. On the planted-pocket
benchmark the tree recovers injected pocket cardinalities exactly (e.g.\
$60$ and $40$ of $60$ and $40$), and the corresponding minimum-spanning-tree
edge statistic separates perfectly ($0$ versus $199$ short edges for a
$200$-point pocket).

\subsection{Complexity}

All continuous-layer statistics run on the reservoir or the pair sample:
the curve and its nulls are $O(P\log P)$ in the pair-sample size $P$; the DTM
field is a blocked Gram top-$k$, $O(n_r^2 d)$ on a capped subsample
($n_r \le 6{,}000$ by default, seconds on CPU); the merge tree is a dense
Prim MST on $n_r \le 4{,}096$ rows plus a linear condensation pass
($\sim0.5$\,s). Nothing in the layer exceeds a few seconds at reservoir
scale, and the full-corpus quantities (second moments, norms) stream in one
pass.

\subsection{Design filters}

Candidate metrics for the layer were admitted or rejected by five tests,
recorded here because they prune future proposals cheaply: (i) \emph{spectrum}
--- does it reduce, under a linear kernel, to a functional of the covariance
spectrum already reported (effective rank, IsoScore)? (ii) \emph{continuity}
--- is it a continuous functional of the data or a step function of
bin/rank/partition membership? (iii) \emph{null} --- does it come with a
calibrated reference? (iv) \emph{naming} --- can it score items, or only
regions? (v) \emph{supervision} --- is it label-free? These filters excluded,
among others, RankMe/NESum/stable rank (spectrum), $k$-means-conditioned
statistics (continuity), raw counts (null), all cell aggregates (naming), and
neural-collapse metrics \cite{papyan2020} and their kin (supervision).

\section{Operational Semantics: the Resolution Bandwidth}
% =========================================================================

Geometric functionals ask ``how uniform is the sphere?''; a practitioner asks
``how much query sloppiness can this corpus absorb?''. This section converts
the former into the latter exactly.

\subsection{An exact pairwise confusion law}

Model a query aimed at item $x$ as $q = x + \sigma g$, $g \sim
\mathcal N(0, I_d)$ --- the simplest honest model of ``the query means $x$ but
lands nearby.'' Retrieval errs on competitor $y$ when
$\langle q, y\rangle > \langle q, x\rangle$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact flip law]\label{prop:flip}
For unit vectors at chord distance $r = \lVert x - y\rVert$,
\[
\Pr\big[\langle q,y\rangle > \langle q,x\rangle\big]
\;=\; \Phi\!\left(-\,\frac{r}{2\sigma}\right),
\]
exactly, in any dimension.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\langle q, y-x\rangle$ is Gaussian with mean $\langle x, y-x\rangle =
\cos\theta - 1 = -r^2/2$ and standard deviation $\sigma\lVert y - x\rVert =
\sigma r$; the flip probability is $\Phi\big((-r^2/2)/(\sigma r)\big)$.
Geometrically: the decision boundary is the perpendicular bisector at distance
$r/2$, and $\Phi(-(r/2)/\sigma)$ is the chance the noise crosses it.
\end{proof}

Simulation at $d = 768$ over $\theta \in [0.1, 2.0]$ and $\sigma \in
[0.05, 0.6]$ matches Proposition~\ref{prop:flip} to a maximum absolute
deviation of $0.001$ (Monte-Carlo error at $4\times10^5$ trials). Two
readings follow: a competitor far relative to the noise is harmless, and a
near-duplicate is a coin flip \emph{at any noise level} --- the formal reason
duplicate pockets are the worst crowding pathology.

\subsection{$C(\sigma)$, $\sstar$, and the uniformity bound}

Summing the law over competitors gives the expected number of items outranking
the intended target,
\[
C(\sigma) \;=\; \sum_{j \ne i} \Phi\!\left(-\frac{r_{ij}}{2\sigma}\right)
\;\;\approx\;\; (n-1)\!\int \Phi\!\left(-\frac{r}{2\sigma}\right) dK(r),
\]
a functional of the pair-distance distribution alone. By Boole's inequality
the sum bounds the probability of \emph{any} error under arbitrary competitor
correlations, so $C$ is conservative: hubness makes it loose, never wrong.

\begin{definition}[Resolution bandwidth]
$\sstar$ is the largest $\sigma$ with $C(\sigma) \le 1$: the most noise
queries can carry before, in expectation, one wrong item outranks the right
one. $C$ is monotone in $\sigma$; bisection inverts it.
\end{definition}

$\sstar$ is reported against the uniform null's crossing at matched $(n,d)$;
the ratio is the fraction of the ideal noise budget the corpus retains, and
the gap is the budget crowding has spent. The per-coordinate scale $\sigma$
translates to intuition through the query's self-similarity decay,
$\langle \bar q, x\rangle \approx (1+\sigma^2 d)^{-1/2}$: $\sstar$ states how
\emph{faded} a query may become against its own target and still be expected
to win.

\begin{proposition}[Uniformity as a Chernoff bound]\label{prop:chernoff}
With $U_t = \log \E\, e^{-t \lVert x - y\rVert^2}$ the uniformity functional
of \cite{wang2020}, $\;C(\sigma) \le (n-1)\, e^{U_t}$ at $t = 1/(8\sigma^2)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\Phi(-z) \le e^{-z^2/2}$ with $z = r/2\sigma$.
\end{proof}

The widely used uniformity temperature is thus not arbitrary: $t{=}2$ is a
collision bound at query noise $\sigma = 0.25$. \textsf{ambit} reports
uniformity with this reading attached.

\subsection{Scope}

The semantics are stated with their boundary: (i) the corpus is treated as its
own query population --- exact for deduplication, clustering, and
corpus-as-queries retrieval; extrapolation to an external workload assumes the
workload lands where the documents are; (ii) the noise channel is isotropic,
while real query drift is structured; (iii) the aggregate is an upper bound.
$\sstar$ is therefore a conservative intra-corpus guarantee, strongest used
comparatively (one corpus embedded two ways, or one embedding over time).

\subsection{Negative results}

Two results from the design process are recorded so they stay dead.

\emph{Near-redundancy of $C$ with uniformity.} Across eight synthetic corpus
types (uniform; cone; one, five, and twenty planted clumps; cone with
duplicates; low-rank collapse), $\log C(\sigma{=}0.25)$ and $U_{t=2}$ agree on
$27$ of $28$ pairwise orderings ($r = 0.978$): as a corpus-level
\emph{discriminative} measurement, the confusion transform adds almost
nothing beyond uniformity --- its value is semantic (units, calibration,
per-item counts). The single disagreement is, however, informative: the
confusion kernel ranks a duplicate-pocket corpus as worse than a diffuse
low-rank collapse, while the Gaussian kernel ranks them oppositely; the
confusion ordering is the operationally correct one, and the flip occurs
precisely between qualitatively different pathologies.

\emph{A vacuous stability certificate.} Composing the DTM stability bound
with a split-half empirical Wasserstein estimate, in the hope of finite-sample
error bars, fails structurally at embedding dimension: the matching distance
between independent halves is of the order of the data scale
($\approx 1.35$ on $\mathbb S^{767}$ at $n_r = 2{,}000$), so the certificate
$m^{-1/2} \widehat W_2 \approx 13.5$ exceeds the field's entire range by an
order of magnitude. Dimension-free certificates would require a different
metric with its own stability theorem --- an open question, not an
engineering task.

\section{The Instrument}
% =========================================================================

\subsection{Architecture}

\textsf{ambit} is a library with a thin CLI. One canonical in-memory type
(\texttt{EmbeddingSet}) flows through a pipeline: a \emph{streaming scan}
(one pass over any of \texttt{.npy/.npz/.parquet/.csv/.jsonl}, single file or
sharded directory) accumulates exact second moments, norms, and a uniform
reservoir; \texttt{build\_ctx} derives projections (PCA by default ---
deterministic and dependency-light), the eigenspectrum, a kNN graph
(optionally filtered to \emph{reciprocal} neighbors, which suppresses hubs by
construction), unsupervised clusters, and the pair-cosine sample; a figure
registry renders a single self-contained, theme-adaptive HTML file with no
external requests. The core depends on \textsf{numpy} alone; heavier
capabilities (tabular IO, ANN graphs, UMAP, GPU paths, on-the-fly embedding
via an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, the training extra) are optional and
degrade gracefully. A comparison mode aligns two embeddings of the same items
by id and prefixes the report with a local neighbor-overlap view read against
global representational-similarity statistics (CKA \cite{kornblith2019},
kernel and rigid distances): the local/global pair can disagree
informatively, a high-CKA/low-overlap outcome being a re-skin that preserves
gross structure while rewriting fine neighborhoods.

\subsection{Report design}

Three principles govern the report. \emph{Direction, not hue}: meaning lives
in positions along annotated axes; good/bad is a direction, and a single
accent color marks the dataset's own signal. \emph{Reference, not threshold}:
every panel draws its null (analytic, simulated, or the
anisotropy-conditioned reference), because absolute cosine is not
self-interpreting. \emph{Display versus measurement}: projections lay figures
out but never carry the statistic; native-space quantities do. The figures
are ordered as a narrative --- \emph{see it} (a density-colored cloud of the
corpus), \emph{how crowded and at what scale} (fingerprint, crowding curve,
bandwidth), \emph{who is affected} (fields ending in named ids),
\emph{what structure} (pockets; separability of provided or discovered
groups, with a provenance line stating which), \emph{why} (spectral
mechanics) --- and every card carries an in-place explainer with an intuition
diagram, plain-language reading rules, and the card's unstated context (null
construction, subsample sizes, id provenance). One legibility finding is
worth recording: a minimum-spanning-tree view drawn over the projection ---
even restricted to tighter-than-bulk bridges --- remained too busy to read on
real corpora and was demoted to a hidden option; the condensed tree carries
the same object legibly as prominence bars.

\subsection{Case study}

A public legal corpus of $10^6$ documents in four subsets, embedded at
$d = 1{,}024$ by an instruction-tuned text encoder, exercises the full
system (report generation: $\sim3.5$ minutes on CPU, single pass).
Table~\ref{tab:case} summarizes. The corpus is strongly cone-shaped (mean
pair cosine $+0.236$; IsoScore $0.090$) yet spectrally rich (effective rank
$728/1024$); the occupancy discrepancy sits at $z = -4{,}211$ with liftoff at
$\cos \approx +0.07$ --- overwhelmingly the cone, per the ACG reference.
Operationally the corpus retains $83\%$ of the ideal noise budget
($\sstar = 0.123$ vs.\ $0.148$), i.e.\ a query may fade to cosine
$\approx 0.25$ against its target before an impostor is expected to win. The
per-item field's low tail is real but shallow (radii $\approx 0.92$ against
a bulk band near $1.4$; $3$--$12$ expected collisions each), and the merge
tree finds one modest pocket ($n = 204$, prominence $0.09$) --- crowding
here is diffuse within subsets rather than pocketed, exactly the regime where
global averages mislead in the \emph{optimistic} direction while per-item
counts still name the at-risk documents. The separability panel (labels
provided) shows high neighborhood purity ($0.95$) against near-zero
silhouette ($+0.04$): subsets are locally coherent yet globally entangled
within the cone --- a reading no single number provides.

\section{From Measurement to Gradient}
% =========================================================================

The confusion kernel is differentiable, so the measurement layer doubles as a
training-time regularizer with a stated guarantee.

\subsection{Losses}

For a batch $z_1,\dots,z_B$ (normalized) and measured target scale $\sigma$:
\[
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{conf}}
= \mathop{\mathrm{mean}}_{(i,j)\,\notin\, \mathcal E}
\Phi\!\left(-\frac{\lVert z_i - z_j\rVert}{2\sigma}\right),
\qquad
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pres}}
= \mathop{\mathrm{mean}}_{i}\;
\mathrm{KL}\!\left(p^{\mathrm{base}}_i \,\middle\|\, p_i\right),
\]
where $\mathcal E$ excludes positives and guarded pairs, and $p_i$
($p^{\mathrm{base}}_i$) are temperature-softmax similarity rows under the
tuned (frozen base) model. $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{conf}}$ is the batch
estimate of $C(\sigma)$; since $\partial \Phi(-r/2\sigma)/\partial r \propto
e^{-r^2/8\sigma^2}$, its gradient vanishes exponentially for pairs beyond
$\approx 3\sigma$ --- far-pair (dissimilarity) structure \emph{cannot} be
disturbed, a property enforced by a unit test (a near pair receives
$>10^3\times$ the gradient of an orthogonal pair). $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pres}}$
is the neighbor-overlap comparison in differentiable form and is the explicit
statement of ``do not rewrite who is similar to whom.'' (An implementation
note: the chord computation floors $2-2c$ before the square root; at exactly
zero the root's infinite derivative poisons masked entries with
$0\cdot\infty$ NaN gradients.)

\subsection{Mining and protocol}

Batch composition is driven by the measurements: sampling weights proportional
to per-item collision counts (with a uniform floor so the bulk anchors the
geometry), and negatives drafted from the \emph{confusable window} --- the
band between the measured liftoff and the alignment scale of true pairs ---
under a false-negative guard: an anchor's top-$m$ base-model neighbors are
never negatives, because the window is precisely where unlabeled true
relatives live. The protocol is staged: diagnose and take the cheapest
licensed fix (cone $\Rightarrow$ linear post-processing; duplicates
$\Rightarrow$ data repair; only genuine beyond-cone clumping licenses
training); train with
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{task}} + \lambda_c \mathcal L_{\mathrm{conf}} +
\lambda_p \mathcal L_{\mathrm{pres}}$ at the measured $\sigma$; verify on a
held-out reservoir never seen by mining, watching $\sstar$ rise, liftoff
retreat, flagged-item collisions fall, and neighbor overlap with the base
stay high (the drift alarm) --- and never grade with the functional that was
optimized. In the worked end-to-end example (a planted $300$-item pocket in
$4{,}000$ items at $d = 64$; a linear adapter as the model stand-in), flagged
items' median expected collisions fall from $2.6$ to $1.6$ at held-out
neighbor overlap $0.93$; we report this as the honest ceiling of a
\emph{linear} adapter --- which cannot unfold a tight pocket further without
moving everything else, exactly the trade the preservation term enforces ---
rather than as the method's ceiling.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
% =========================================================================

\textbf{External validation.} The instrument's readouts are geometrically
grounded and internally verified; the outstanding scientific step is a study
correlating them with measured retrieval outcomes across corpora and
encoders, using relevance judgments only to \emph{score} the unsupervised
predictions. The confusion transform makes one falsifiable prediction its
near-redundant sibling does not --- duplicate pathology outranks diffuse
collapse --- which such a study would test directly.
\textbf{Query distributions.} Accepting an optional \emph{unlabeled} query
set (no judgments; within the tool's supervision constraint) would replace
the corpus-as-queries assumption with a measured cross-set confusion.
\textbf{Sharper aggregates.} The union bound is safe but loose where hubs
live; Gaussian comparison inequalities may yield two-sided estimates.
\textbf{Recoverability.} ``Resolution headroom'' --- the fraction of measured
crowding removable by a budgeted transform class, computed rather than
predicted --- would answer the practitioner's first question (``is this the
embedding's fault or my data's?'') and is well-posed but unproven off the
reservoir. \textbf{Streaming structure.} The pair curve sketches trivially;
the merge tree does not; a mergeable approximate hierarchy with prominence
error bounds would extend the structural layer past the reservoir.

% =========================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% =========================================================================

Crowding is where embedded systems quietly fail, and it is exactly the
property that global averages cannot see and binned displays cannot be
trusted to measure. The instrument described here rests on one methodological
commitment applied uniformly: \emph{read continuous objects at every scale
against stated nulls, and descend to named items}. The mathematics is
classical --- Ripley's curve, Stolarsky's identity, the distance to a
measure, the condensed cluster tree, a Gaussian tail bound --- and the
contribution is their selection, calibration, and composition into a system
with stated guarantees and stated scope, plus the operational layer that
converts geometry into a noise budget a practitioner can act on. The same
mathematics, being differentiable, closes the loop from diagnosis to repair
with a gradient that provably cannot disturb what it is not aimed at. The
negative results are reported alongside the positive ones because an
instrument is trustworthy only when its designers say what it cannot do.
